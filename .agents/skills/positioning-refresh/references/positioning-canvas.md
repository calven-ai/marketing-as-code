# Positioning elements and the use-case canvas

Condensed from the positioning and messaging designer in the B2B RevOps
skills. Use it as a checklist when you test `strategy/positioning.md`; the
headings there already map onto these elements.

## The chain

ICP → positioning → messaging framework → copy. A refresh that skips a link
breaks everything downstream, which is why this skill lists the cascade.

## Twelve elements to check

Primary (they answer "why choose you"):

1. Category: the frame of reference the buyer already understands.
2. Alternative: what they would use instead (often the status quo, not a
   vendor).
3. Differentiation: the one thing the alternative cannot do.
4. Capabilities: what a customer can now do.
5. Benefits: the result of those capabilities.
6. Features: the technical enablers, listed last.

Secondary (they add specificity):

7. Personas: who benefits most.
8. Use case: the job being hired for.
9. Overarching problem: the big-picture struggle, in customer words.
10. to 12. Three sub-problems: the specific blockers, each from an interview
    or a transcript, never invented.

## Six positioning-line formulas

| Formula | Shape |
| --- | --- |
| Problem and outcome | We help [who] reduce [pain] so they [result] |
| Job to be done | When you need to [job], use [us] instead of [old way] |
| Category plus alternative | Unlike [alternative], we are [difference] |
| Segment or niche | [Category] built for [niche], not generic [everyone] |
| Primary value vector | The most [value] way for [who] to [result] |
| Strategic archetype | The [archetype] in [category] for [who] |

Pick one, keep it repeatable, back it with proof, and use the same line in
every channel. Ten variants generated in seconds are worth nothing until one
is tested against real buyers.

## The use-case canvas

Fill the left side first, in the customer's own words, then flip each row.

| Old way (their reality) | Center | New way (what becomes possible) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Overarching problem | Use case | Desired outcome |
| Three specific problems | Persona | Benefits, one per problem |
| Limitation of the current way | | Capability |
| Current way, the anti-feature | | Product feature |

Every right-side cell is the opposite of a left-side cell. If you cannot
state the contrast, the differentiation is not real yet.

## Tests before you propose the diff

- Thirty-second clarity: a stranger understands who you are for and against
  what after thirty seconds.
- Pain language: the left side sounds like a quote, specific and concrete,
  not "better visibility".
- Evidence prerequisite: no interviews, transcripts or deal analysis with
  pain language means the positioning is fiction; say so and stop.

## Adoption signals to look for after a change

- Eight of ten reps say the core positioning on recorded calls.
- Discovery reaches "I understand what you do" inside three minutes.
- Objections move from awareness ("are you like X?") to product ("does it
  work with our CRM?").
- Sales leadership repeats the line without notes.

## Common mistakes

Writing positioning before the interviews; filling the right side of the
canvas first; marketing language instead of customer words; skipping the
contrast; treating the document as final instead of reviewed on a cadence.
