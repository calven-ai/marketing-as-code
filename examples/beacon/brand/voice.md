# Voice and tone

## The voice in three adjectives

Plain, calm, on your side. Plain: short words, no jargon a customer would
have to look up. Calm: an outage is not the end of the world, and our
writing should not sound like it is. On your side: we write from the
support team's chair, never from the vendor's.

## How we write

- **Sentences:** short and declarative. One idea each.
- **Person:** "we" for Beacon, "you" for the reader, never "users".
- **Jargon:** "incident", "status page" and "postmortem" are fine, the
  audience says them; "observability", "SLA breach" and "downstream" are
  engineering words, avoid them.
- **Humor:** dry, rare, never about a customer's outage.
- **Claims:** every number has a source we can name. No superlatives.

## Tone by context

| Context | Tone shifts to |
| --- | --- |
| Blog / thought leadership | Opinionated, from experience, with one example per point |
| Product announcements | Matter of fact: what changed, who it helps, where to click |
| Support / incident comms | Slower and warmer; say what we know, what we do not, and when we will say more |
| Social | Same voice, shorter; a question beats a claim |

## Do / don't examples

| Don't write | Write instead | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Beacon revolutionizes incident communication with seamless multi-channel orchestration. | When something breaks, your customers hear it from you first. | The reader is a support lead, not an analyst. |
| Users are notified via configurable notification channels. | The customers who were affected get the update. The others do not. | "Users" is nobody; the benefit is the audience. |
| We apologize for any inconvenience this may have caused. | We know this cost you an afternoon. Here is what happened and what we changed. | An apology template sounds like one. |

## Banned list

revolutionize, seamless, leverage, best-in-class, "users", "any
inconvenience".
