# Launch review windows and ranges

## What is measurable when

| Week | Check |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | signups and conversion by channel; onboarding completion; early churn signals; top feedback themes; announcement email opens and clicks; social engagement; critical bugs or blockers; Product Hunt or listing rank where used |
| 2 | cumulative signups and trajectory; activation rate (the ontology's activation action); sequence opens and clicks; organic versus paid traffic split; support ticket volume and themes; repeat engagement |
| 4 | trial-to-paid (or signup-to-opportunity for sales-led); acquisition cost by channel; 30-day retention; feature usage distribution; NPS or satisfaction trend; content performance (blog, webinar, demo); competitor reaction; revenue impact where any |
| 8 | cohort retention curves; expansion revenue; early lifetime-value signals; channel efficiency ranking; organic growth (referrals, word of mouth); brand mentions; product-market-fit indicators; recommendations for the next launch |

A retro before week 4 reports tiers 1 and 2 and says tier 3 is immature;
a week-8 retro is the one that ranks channels.

## Ranges, for orientation only

| Metric | Published range | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| landing page conversion | 10 to 20% | targeting quality decides |
| announcement email open rate | 40 to 60% | engaged lists |
| social engagement rate | 2 to 5% | per platform |
| onboarding completion | 60 to 80% | SaaS average nearer 50% |
| 7-day retention | 40 to 60% | early-stage products |
| 30-day retention | 20 to 40% | category-dependent |
| trial to paid | 5 to 15% | B2B often higher |
| NPS | 30 or more shows traction; 50 or more is strong | |
| CAC payback | 6 to 12 months | should improve after launch |

The team's own previous launch is the real baseline; cite a range only
to say a number is unusual.

## Channel types

- Owned (site, email, product, community): 30 to 50% of sustained
  traffic after launch, improving over time.
- Rented (social platforms, marketplaces, listings): 60 to 80% of
  launch-day traffic, decaying fast without owned follow-up.
- Borrowed (partners, press, influencers): credibility and a one-time
  spike; conversion depends on audience fit.

A launch whose traffic was mostly rented and has no owned follow-up
shows the decay by week 2; the retro says so and the playbook records
the owned sequence to add.

## Launch tiers, so the retro compares like with like

Internal (friendly users), alpha (controlled early access), beta (scaled
testing), early access (controlled expansion with quantitative data),
full (open signup, every channel). The brief names the tier; the retro
judges against that tier's goals, not the full-launch ones.
