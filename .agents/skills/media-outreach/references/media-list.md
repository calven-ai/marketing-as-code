# Building the list and the relationship

Condensed from the source above.

## Relationship levels (the `relationship` column)

| Level | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| cold | never interacted |
| warm | the team has engaged with their work |
| connected | messages exchanged |
| trusted | the team has been a useful source |
| go-to | they reach out first |

A journalist at trusted or go-to is pitched by the person who holds the
relationship, with a note from you, not from the list.

## The 10:1 rule

For every pitch, ten value-adds first: share their article with a real
comment; send a tip on their beat; introduce a source; answer a source
request even when it is not about you; give background off the record;
offer early access; share data before it is public; be a source for a
story you are not in.

## Building the list, three to six months before a launch

Months 1 to 2: identify 20 to 30 journalists on the beat, read their last
pieces, follow them. Months 2 to 3: share their work, answer source
requests, send tips, open light conversations. Months 3 to 6: offer to be
a background source, make introductions, be a recognisable presence.

## Tracking fields

Name, outlet, beat, last interaction date, preferences, relationship
level, next value-add. In this repo these live in the media-list snapshot
(private repo) and in a `memory/knowledge/` relationships file.

## Channels

Email for pitches and detail; a social DM for quick tips; LinkedIn to
connect but never to pitch at once; text or Signal only when the
relationship warrants it; in person at events, respecting their time.

## After coverage

Same day, thank them; same week, share the piece and tag them; ongoing,
keep adding value unrelated to the company; never ask for a change after
publication. On a job change: congratulate, offer to be useful on the
beat, do not pivot to a pitch.

## Source requests

Reply within hours; answer exactly what was asked; be quotable and
specific; credentials in one line; do not send the whole company
overview; do not demand to review quotes.

## Anti-patterns

Transactional-only contact; claimed familiarity; guilt about past
support; pitches disguised as favours; the wrong beat; public pressure
by tagging; more than one or two touches a month without a reason.
