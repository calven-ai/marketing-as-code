# Fernhill case study interview

**Tomas Berg:** Thanks for doing this. Can you start with what an incident looked like before Beacon?

**Joanna Park:** Someone in engineering would post in Slack that something was down, and then I'd write the customer update by hand. Same message to everyone, by email and on the status page. Half the people who got it weren't affected, and a lot of them opened a ticket to ask if they were.

**Tomas Berg:** And now?

**Joanna Park:** We split customers by region and by plan. When the EU export service went down in April, only the EU customers on the plans with export heard about it. The update went out in about two minutes, from a template we'd written in our own words.

**Tomas Berg:** You told us tickets dropped. Can you put a number on it?

**Joanna Park:** We compared the quarter before we switched with our first full quarter on Beacon. Incident tickets were down 31 percent. Same number of incidents, roughly. The difference is people who weren't affected stopped asking.

**Tomas Berg:** Can we use the 31 percent with your name on it?

**Joanna Park:** I think so, but my Head of Support has to approve anything with numbers, and our comms team checks quotes. Send them to me and I'll route them.

**Tomas Berg:** Of course. What about the report afterwards?

**Joanna Park:** That's the part I didn't expect to care about. After each incident we send the post-incident report, and my Head of Support forwards it to our leadership as it is. Before, I'd spend an afternoon rewriting the engineering postmortem into something a customer could read. Our account managers forward it to the big accounts too.

**Tomas Berg:** Would you say that on a webinar? We're doing one on the twelfth of November, "The first ten minutes of an outage".

**Joanna Park:** Happy to, same approval applies. Send me the date and the questions.
