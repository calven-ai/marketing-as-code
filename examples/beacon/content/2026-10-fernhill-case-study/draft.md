# How Fernhill cut incident tickets by 31 percent

**Quotes below are pending approval by Joanna Park and Fernhill. Do not
publish or share outside Beacon until both have signed off.**

Fernhill makes a design tool that its customers keep open all day. When
it goes down, they notice in seconds. The company has 180 people and a
support team that, until this year, found out about most incidents from
the customers themselves.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Company | Fernhill, design tool, 180 people |
| Beacon customer since | February 2026 |
| Team | Support, led during incidents by Joanna Park, Support Team Lead |
| Result | 31 percent fewer incident tickets after one quarter |

## Before: one page, one list, every customer

Fernhill's status page came with its monitoring tool. Engineering set it
up and owned it. When something broke, Joanna's team wrote the customer
emails by hand, to the whole list, while the queue filled up.

> "The page told people something was down. It did not tell them whether
> it was them. So they asked us, all of them, at once."
> Joanna Park, Support Team Lead, Fernhill *(pending approval)*

Most of those tickets came from customers in regions or plans the
incident never touched.

## What they changed

Fernhill kept its status page and moved the customer conversation to
Beacon. Joanna's team wrote its templates once, in Fernhill's voice, and
built audience segments from the regions and plans already in their CRM.

During an incident, the support lead on shift now picks the segments
affected and sends one update. It reaches the status page, a banner in
the product and the inbox of the customers concerned. Nobody else hears
about it.

> "The first update stopped being the hard part. We open the template,
> pick who it is for, and send it. Then we can get back to the queue."
> Joanna Park *(pending approval)*

## The report they forward

The change Joanna did not expect was the morning after. Beacon drafts a
customer-facing report from the incident timeline; the team edits it and
sends it to the affected accounts.

> "Our account managers used to dread the call after an outage. Now the
> customer has already forwarded our report to their boss, and the call
> starts somewhere else."
> Joanna Park *(pending approval)*

## The result

In the first full quarter on Beacon, ticket volume during incidents fell
31 percent, measured in Fernhill's own support platform against the
quarter before.

> "We did not get fewer incidents. We got fewer tickets about them, and
> the ones we got were from customers who were actually affected."
> Joanna Park *(pending approval)*
