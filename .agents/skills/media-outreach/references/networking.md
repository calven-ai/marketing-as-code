# The research pass and the first message

Condensed from the source above; applied here to journalists, podcast
hosts and editors.

## Research before drafting

Current role, outlet and headline; two or three recent pieces and their
themes; what the outlet is pushing right now (a series, a vertical, an
event); career path; talks and interviews; shared communities or people;
something they are visibly building or excited about.

## Value-add angles

Share a relevant resource; react to a piece with a real insight; extend
or challenge their argument; offer a complementary perspective; introduce
a source; flag an opportunity; share a related result; give feedback on
something they built; ask a precise expert question; surface a shared
interest.

## The first email

Subject: two to four lowercase words that look internal. Body: a specific
hook about their work; one sentence connecting it to why you write; what
you are offering; a soft ask ("worth a look?"). Under 200 words for a
relationship note, under 150 for a pitch. No "hope this finds you well",
no "I am reaching out because", a two-to-one ratio of "you" to "I", one
ask, and never a favour in the first message.

## The connection request

Under 300 characters: a specific observation about their work, one line
on why to connect, an optional soft opener. Never "pick your brain".

## Authenticity

Lead with their world; personalise to real points, not filler; combine a
specific observation with substance; one low-friction CTA; the sender
adjusts it to their own voice before it goes out.
