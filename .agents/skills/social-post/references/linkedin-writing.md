# Writing a LinkedIn post

Condensed from the content-creation mode of the linkedin skill in
manojbajaj95/claude-gtm-plugin. Profile auditing was left out.

## Principles

- Authenticity over performance: readers spot manufactured
  vulnerability and engagement bait at once. Real experience, specific
  insight, admitting what you do not know.
- One idea per post.
- Value without strings: would a stranger find this useful?

## Anatomy and limits

Hook (first one or two lines, about 210 characters visible before "see
more"), body in one-or-two-sentence paragraphs with blank lines, CTA in
the last line or two, three to five hashtags at the very end. Post text
up to 3,000 characters; a comment up to 1,250.

## Hook types

| Type | Shape |
| --- | --- |
| Honest admission | "I've been wrong about X." |
| Specific observation | "I've noticed one thing in every team that did Y." |
| Direct challenge | "Most advice on X optimises for the wrong thing." |
| Unexpected angle | "The best hire I made had the worst CV." |
| Contrarian opinion | "Unpopular opinion: X is a waste of time." |
| Personal story | "I got fired on a Tuesday. Best thing that happened." |
| List promise | "I've reviewed 200 briefs. Five red flags." |

Retire: "This one thing made me $X", "The CEO pulled me aside",
"I'm excited to announce", "In today's rapidly evolving landscape",
opening with a hashtag or an emoji.

## Formats

- Story: admission or surprising outcome, one line of context, the
  tension, the turning point, the lesson, a question.
- List: a hook with a clear promise, why it matters in one line, three
  to seven numbered points with a line of context each, a closing
  insight or question.
- Contrarian: the position stated, the belief challenged, the
  reasoning, evidence, a nuanced close, an invitation. Guardrails: real
  expertise, argue against ideas not people, offer an alternative, be
  open to being wrong.
- Observation: what you saw, specific evidence, why it matters, your
  reading, a question to test whether others see it too.

## Pillars to rotate

Expertise, stories, opinions, behind the scenes, curated insight.

## What the feed rewards and punishes

Rewards dwell time (posts people read to the end), comments (ask real
questions), saves (reference-worthy), "see more" clicks (a strong hook).
Punishes external links in the body (put them in the first comment and
say so), editing in the first hour, more than one post a day.

## CTAs

A genuine question, an agreement check, an experience ask, a bookmark
prompt. Never "comment YES if", "share with three people", "follow for
more".

## Sharing something personal

Only when it taught something others can use, you have processed it,
and it serves the reader; never when the wound is fresh or the aim is
sympathy. Test: helping others, or processing your own feelings? Fine if
it went viral?

## Anti-patterns

Corporate announcement speak; humblebrags as lessons; recycled viral
templates; every sentence on its own line, always; hashtag stuffing;
engagement pods.

## Pre-posting checklist

- Hook: would it stop my scroll?
- Focus: one clear idea?
- Value: useful if someone else posted it?
- Authenticity: sounds like the person, not "LinkedIn them"?
- Format: short paragraphs, scannable?
- CTA: a real question, not bait?
- Links: in the comment, not the body?
