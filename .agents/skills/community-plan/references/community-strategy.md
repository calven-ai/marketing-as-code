# Community strategy: go or no-go, platform, rituals, health

## Start when, do not start when

Start when: 50 or more engaged users or customers who would take part;
the product is tied to an identity, not only a utility; someone can put
in ten or more hours a week for management and seeding; the business goal
matches what a community produces (retention, word of mouth, product
feedback); there is a clear answer to "what do members get from each
other that they cannot get alone".

Do not start when: the community is meant as a marketing channel rather
than a member benefit; nobody has bandwidth for months one to three; the
product is transactional with no repeat interaction.

## Platform by how members talk

| Platform | Fits | Trade-off |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Discord | developers, creators, real time | noisy; steep onboarding |
| Slack | B2B professionals, familiar | history paywalled at scale; can feel transactional |
| Circle | courses, creators, a branded space | needs traffic driven to it |
| Discourse | technical, long form, searchable | slower adoption; posting friction |
| Reddit | public, high volume, indexed | no ownership; hard to moderate |
| Facebook groups | consumer, older demographics | reach decays with the algorithm |

Match the platform's speed to the members' communication style.

## Principles

Identity over product: members join for the solution and stay for who
they become; name the shared identity. Value flows to members first:
knowledge, peers, recognition, product influence, visibility. The
flywheel: join, get value, engage, help others, which brings new members.

## Launch, in phases

1. Founding members (weeks 1 to 2): 20 to 50 recruited by hand; do not
   open publicly before there is activity.
2. Seed the culture (weeks 2 to 3): guidelines that describe the tone,
   five to ten posts modelling behaviour, a founding call.
3. Do what does not scale (weeks 1 to 8): reply to every post, welcome
   each member by name, a weekly ritual.
4. The core loop: the one weekly action ("share what you are working
   on", "ask in the help channel", "post a win"), easy and publicly
   rewarded.

## Rituals

Weekly: a "what are you working on" thread, office hours or a Q&A, a
wins thread. Monthly: an expert or founder AMA, a themed challenge,
recognition. Seasonal: a hackathon, a contest, an annual gathering.
Predictability is the point.

## Moderation

Rules: respect, relevance, authenticity (no value-free self-promotion),
helpfulness. Playbook: warn first-time offenders, remove spam within 24
hours, highlight exemplary posts, give top contributors private feedback
and early moderator input. Reinforce: showcase member wins, credit
members whose feedback shipped, badges or roles for high contributors.

## Health, weekly

| Metric | Healthy | Red flag |
| --- | --- | --- |
| daily to monthly active ratio | over 20% | under 10% |
| new members posting within 7 days | over 30% | under 15% |
| threads that get a reply | over 60% | under 40% |
| inactive 30 days or more | under 20% | over 40% |
| posts not from staff | over 70% | under 50% |

Weekly questions: are questions answered within 24 hours; is the same 5%
producing 80% of the engagement (invest in them); do new members post
after joining (if not, fix onboarding).

## Growing an existing community

Find the leaky stage (join, intro, first post, regular); a pinned start
here, an introductions channel with warm replies, a personal welcome, a
first-week prompt; surface member wins; invest in the 1% who create most
of the value. Ambassadors: pick people already recommending the product,
approach one to one, offer early access, recognition, a private channel,
referral links and assets; measure referral signups, engagement against
the baseline, and feedback that shipped.
