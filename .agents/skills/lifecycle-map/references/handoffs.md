# Lifecycle handoffs: where automated email belongs, and the usual gaps

Condensed from the handoffs skill above. Stage names here are the
generic ones; `data/ontology/funnel.md` decides what they are called and
when they happen for this team.

## The five handoffs and the email that should sit on each

| Handoff | Trigger | Response expectation | Automated touch |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Marketing to sales | hand-raiser, MQL or PQL created | under 5 minutes for a hand-raiser or paid lead; 10 minutes organic; 30 minutes partner; days for content leads | confirmation to the lead; alert to the rep; escalation if unworked in 24 hours |
| Sales to onboarding or partner | closed won | internal briefing within 24 to 48 hours; customer intro in week one; kickoff 48 to 72 hours after signature | welcome from the new owner; kickoff invite; the goals and commitments restated |
| Onboarding to success | go-live | success owns from go-live; hypercare 2 to 4 weeks | "you are live" with the first success metric; training gaps addressed |
| Success to expansion | usage at 80% of a limit, a new stakeholder, a usage spike | expansion deal within 48 hours of the signal | feature nudges tied to usage; a briefing to the AE |
| Lifecycle marketing, every stage | any stage change | triggered by the stage change, never by a calendar | onboarding days 1 to 14; adoption weeks 2 to 8; NPS at months 3, 6, 12; expansion nudges on usage; advocacy asks to champions |

Speed matters at the first handoff: a reply inside five minutes
qualifies far more often than one after an hour, and MQL conversion with
scoring runs 39 to 40% against 15 to 21% without.

## The gaps that recur

1. Unowned expansion signals: usage triggers a nudge but no owner.
2. Promise amnesia: success never sees what sales committed to; the
   onboarding email restates it or nobody does.
3. Post-signature silence: 2 to 7 days between closed won and the first
   touch.
4. Lifecycle content exists but is not triggered on the stage change
   (the sequence is manual, or the trigger is a list, not the event).
5. Renewal invisible: the renewal sequence starts at 60 days when the
   account needed 120.
6. New buying group forced through the upsell pipeline; the emails
   assume the original champion.

## What to check on every row of the map

- Trigger is the ontology event or CRM property, exact name.
- Exit fires on the stage change, not only on a click.
- Suppression covers customers, open opportunities and the other
  sequences that could fire the same week.
- The owner of the reply is a person or a queue, named.
- The linked content is published and still true.

## Compliance and consent

A stage change never overrides consent: marketing sequences need the
subscription the tool records; transactional and onboarding emails
follow the tool's transactional rules. Note the subscription type on
each row.
