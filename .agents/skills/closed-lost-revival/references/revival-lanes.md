# The three lanes, the gates, the cap

Condensed from the source above.

## Why bother

About four of five B2B opportunities close lost (win rates near 19 percent
in 2025), post-proposal deals reopen at 31 to 50 percent, and roughly a
fifth of champions change jobs in a year. The lost list is the warmest
cold list the team has, if it is worked with evidence.

## Lane A: closed lost and proposal gone quiet

- Window: 6 to 18 months since close. Younger deals are still fresh
  rejections; older ones are a new conversation.
- Gate: the original objection is recorded and the last contact date is
  known.
- Opener angle: name the objection from the first cycle and the concrete
  thing that changed since (a shipped feature, a price change, their
  funding, their new leader).
- Follow-up window: one, after 7 days.

## Lane B: champion moved

- Window: days 14 to 45 in the new role (settled in, not yet locked into
  a vendor).
- Gate: live employment verified (not a cached enrichment row); the new
  company's profile shows the origin company in the person's history (the
  wrong-person guard); the champion came from a won or genuinely engaged
  deal, not one that declined within 90 days; the new employer is not a
  competitor, an exclusion, or below the minimum company size.
- Opener angle: the prior relationship in terms of what they used and
  achieved, and the specific relevance of the new role.
- Follow-up window: one, after 10 days.

## Lane C: engaged then silent

- Window: 60 days or more of silence (under 60 belongs to normal cadence
  management, not revival).
- Gate: real engagement on record, such as a substantive reply or an
  attended meeting.
- Classify the silence: ghosted, objection pending, timing miss, changed
  landscape. The class picks the opener.
- Opener angle: acknowledge the gap and anchor on their last question.
- Follow-up window: one, after 14 days.

## Hard gates, applied before any lane

Do-not-contact flags; peers, advisors and partners; accounts that never
replied at all; a fundamental ICP or product mismatch; anything the team
logged as "leave it" in the decision log.

## Loss-pattern library

Map each lost account to a loss category (price, timing, integration,
incumbent, champion left, no decision). Reuse the play that worked for the
category; when no category fits, create one and file this account as its
first worked example. Keep the library in `memory/knowledge/` so the tenth
account learns from the nine before it.

## Compression rules

- 5 to 15 accounts per quarter, no more.
- Rank: champion movers, then quiet proposals, then engaged-then-quiet,
  then old closed-lost.
- Every entry has a one-line premise citing dated evidence; undated
  premises do not ship.
- One re-open per account per cycle; one follow-up; then passive nurture.
- Quarterly, not monthly.
- A human approves each approach; nothing is sent autonomously.
