# Decision-driver taxonomy

Open this at step 6, before coding any deal. Code every deal against these seven drivers, a primary and a secondary each, so periods compare. Single-cause coding recreates the dropdown problem: CRM loss fields cluster on "price" and "timing", while buyers name trust, capability gaps and champion strength.

| Driver | Covers | Sounds like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Product capability gap | A thing they needed that you do not do, or do worse | "We needed it to work with our ticketing tool" |
| Trust and proof | References, security, viability, credibility of claims | "We could not find a customer our size" |
| Price to value | Not "too expensive" but value not believed at that price | "Hard to justify next to what we already pay for" |
| Champion strength | Nobody inside carried it, or the champion left or lost | "Our VP moved on and nobody picked it up" |
| Competitive move | A rival's specific action, offer or relationship | "They threw it in with the renewal" |
| Timing and budget reality | Budget vanished, priorities moved, no critical event | "It is not a priority until next year" |
| Process failure | Your sales process: slow, wrong people, weak demo, missed steps | "We never got the security answers in time" |

## How to code

- **Evidence order:** the buyer's own words (call transcript, interview, email) first; the rep's notes second; the CRM loss reason last. Record which you used for each deal.
- **Interviewer framing is not evidence.** In an interview or debrief, take the driver from what the buyer said, not from the interviewer's summary.
- **Built in-house or kept the status quo** is a no-decision outcome, not a competitor win. Code its driver (usually timing and budget reality, or price to value) like any other.
- **Silence is not an answer.** A deal with no notes, no transcript and no reason is "uncoded". Never infer a driver from deal size, stage or rival alone.
- **Wins too.** Code won deals when evidence exists: wins reveal the differentiators that actually decide.

## Reading the result

- Compare the share of each driver in the CRM reasons with the share in the coded evidence. A real divergence is the most useful finding a win/loss read produces.
- A finding that only confirms what the team already believes deserves a second look at the evidence before it ships.
- A good period produces at least one finding that contradicts what the company believes about itself.
