# Interviews and surveys

Condensed from the source above. Use it to design the survey or the
interview guide the team asked for; the sending is theirs.

## Keep it casual

Frame a call as a conversation, not a study. The aim is disconfirmation:
break the assumption, do not validate it. Avoid the words "research
study" in the invite.

## Interview guide

Open with how the product area is actually working for them: what is
good, what is annoying, what they wish were different. Then ladder with
five whys from a concrete event:

1. "Why did you [downgrade / switch / stop using X] last quarter?"
2. "Why weren't you using [the thing the answer names]?"
3. "Why didn't [the fix they expected] happen?"
4. "Why did that matter enough to [take the action]?"
5. "Why is that the real risk for you?"

Ladder pains ("what is broken, what are you paying to escape?") and
passions ("what would you hate to lose?") separately. Close every call
with "who else should we talk to?".

## Recruiting

Shortlist from the CRM by deal size, short sales cycle and long
retention; ask sales and success for referrals. Ten calls is the target;
themes saturate by call five or six. The source's incentive: about 50
dollars per 30-minute call, offered up front; about 5 dollars per survey
response, paid on submit.

Invite shape: one clear ask, two time options, a booking link, the
incentive named, no pitch.

## The survey

- As short as it can be; every extra question lowers completion.
- Open questions first; multiple choice constrains answers to your list.
- No leading wording.
- One product-market-fit question: "How would you feel if you could no
  longer use [product]?" with very disappointed, somewhat disappointed,
  not disappointed, and no longer use it. The source's threshold: 40
  percent "very disappointed" reads as fit.
- Three follow-ups: who would benefit most (sharpens the ICP), the main
  benefit they get (positioning language), what would improve it
  (roadmap input, mainly from the "somewhat" group).

Segment every answer by the "very disappointed" cohort against the rest;
that cohort is the real market.

## Sales safari, before designing anything

Read forums, reviews and threads for pains, jargon, recommendations and
worldview first, so the questions use the customers' words.

## Checklists

Before interviews: CRM segmented, referrals requested, invite
personalised, calendar link tested, incentive ready, ladder drafted per
segment, note-taker assigned.

Before a survey: PMF question in, three follow-ups written, open
questions prioritised, leading language removed, incentive configured,
segmentation plan set, distribution channel confirmed.

After: every answer coded to the synthesis frame, pains and passions
separated, language compiled, confidence labelled, findings segmented,
the next round recruited from "who else?".
