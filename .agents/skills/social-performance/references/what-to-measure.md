# What to measure on social, and what to do about it

Condensed from the analytics section of coreyhaines31/marketingskills
`social`. Terms mean what `data/ontology/` says; engagement is not a
lead.

## Metrics by goal

| Goal | Metrics |
| --- | --- |
| Awareness | impressions, reach, follower growth rate |
| Engagement | engagement rate, comments (worth more than likes), reposts, saves |
| Conversion | link clicks, profile visits, direct messages received, attributed leads (per the ontology, never assumed) |

## The review, every period

- Top three posts and why they worked (hook type, format, topic, time).
- Bottom three and what they teach.
- Follower growth trend.
- Engagement rate trend.
- Best posting times, from the data, not from folklore.

## Cuts that turn numbers into knowledge

Median (not mean; one viral post skews a mean) engagement rate by:
format (text, document, image, video, thread), hook type, pillar or
topic, CTA type, weekday and hour, company voice versus a named person.
A cut with fewer than three posts is reported with its count and never
generalised.

## If engagement is low

Test new hooks; post at different times; try other formats; engage more
with others' posts, since comments given tend to bring comments back.

## If reach is declining

Keep external links out of the post body (first comment instead); post
more consistently; reply in the comments in the first hour; test visual
or video formats.

## Reverse engineering what worked

For a post that outperformed: the hook type, the structure, the length,
the format, the time, what the CTA asked, which pillar it served. The
pattern, not the post, is what goes into the knowledge file
(`what-resonates-on-linkedin.md` in `memory/knowledge/`), with the
report that showed it and the sample size.

## Scheduling notes worth keeping

Schedule evergreen and educational posts; post live for reactions to
news and for anything that needs a reply within the hour. Keep a queue
of two weeks and review it weekly against the calendar.
