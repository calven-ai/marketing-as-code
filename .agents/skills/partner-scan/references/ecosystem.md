# Ecosystem map: which partner types fit which motion, and what to expect

Condensed from the source above.

## Five questions before scanning

1. The problem: reach, credibility, delivery capacity, a new geography or
   vertical, or stickiness through integrations.
2. The motion: product-led points at technology partners; sales-assisted
   at referral and co-selling; enterprise at integrators and consultancies;
   channel-heavy at resellers and MSPs.
3. The stage: under $5M ARR, three to five informal partnerships; $5 to
   15M, a first referral programme with 5 to 10 partners; $15 to 50M, a
   tiered programme with co-selling; above, an ecosystem strategy.
4. Direct motion maturity: partners amplify what exists, broken or not.
5. An owner: a programme without one dies in six months.

## Partner types by motion

| Motion | Primary | Secondary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| product-led | technology (integrations) | marketplaces |
| sales-assisted | referral partners | consultancies, technology |
| enterprise | integrators and consultancies | regional resellers |
| new geography | regional resellers | local consultancies |
| new vertical | vertical specialists | industry integrators |
| implementation-heavy | implementation partners | training partners |

## Three overlaps to look for

Prospect-customer (our target is their customer: the warmest intro);
customer-customer (a shared customer: co-expansion, a joint case study);
prospect-prospect (both target the account: coordinate). Build these
from `data/accounts/target-accounts.csv`, the customers snapshot and the
customer names each candidate publishes; a proper account-mapping tool
comes later.

## Prioritising an overlap

Our deal stage (active pipeline before target list); their relationship
depth; combined deal value; ICP fit for both sides. Deduplicate: about a
third of partner-submitted accounts are duplicates.

## What partners are worth, with their sources

Partner-involved deals win 11.7 percent more often on average, up to 37
percent with many partners (Crossbeam, 2024); they are 53 percent more
likely to close and close 46 percent faster (Crossbeam, 2023); 32 to 60
percent larger (Introw, 2024, and others); partner-involved accounts churn
58 percent less (Crossbeam, 2023); partner CAC around $150 against $1,200
direct (2026 vendor figures). Treat these as the case for a programme,
not as forecasts for this team.

## Programme benchmarks by age

| Metric | under 18 months | 18 to 36 | 36 plus |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| partner-sourced revenue | 5 to 10 percent | 15 to 25 | 25 to 40 plus |
| partner-influenced revenue | 5 to 15 percent | 15 to 25 | 20 to 30 |
| activation (active within 90 days) | above 60 percent | 60 to 75 | 75 to 85 |
| time to first partner deal | 90 to 120 days | 60 to 90 | 30 to 60 |

## Attribution to define before the first campaign

Partner-sourced (they originated it; first meeting via their intro; a
90-day protection window) and partner-influenced (we originated; they
accelerated). Tag at origin in the CRM, never retroactively; first
registration wins.

## Cadence once partners exist

Monthly: refresh the overlap map with the top five partners. Quarterly:
all active partners, new ones onboarded. Yearly: which produced pipeline;
recruit or retire.
