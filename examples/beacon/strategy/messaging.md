# Messaging

The seven sections match the Messaging document a marketing context layer
serves. Two pillars are filled; the third waits for the next customer
study. Words we use and words we avoid live in the voice guide.

## Core Narrative & One-Liner

- **Core message:** When something breaks, your customers hear it from you
  first, clearly, in your voice.
- **One-liner:** Beacon is the incident communication platform for SaaS
  support teams.
- **Elevator pitch (30s):** Every SaaS company has outages. The ones that
  keep their customers are the ones that explain them well: fast, in the
  right place, to the people affected, and with a report the customer can
  forward to their boss. That is a communication job, and the status page
  that came with your monitoring tool was not built for it. Beacon is. One
  update goes to the status page, the product and the inbox at once, in
  your voice, and the report writes itself from what happened.
- **Tagline variants:** "Bad news, well told." / "Your customers hear it
  from you first." / "The outage is not the problem. The silence is."

## Objection Handling

| Objection | Reframe + proof |
| --- | --- |
| "We already have a status page in Northstar." | You have a page. You do not have the update, the audience or the report; Northstar customers who added Beacon kept the page and stopped writing emails by hand. |
| "Our incidents are rare." | Rare is when it matters most: nobody has practised. Templates mean the first update is ready before anyone is calm. |

## Boilerplate

Beacon is the incident communication platform for SaaS support teams. One
update reaches the status page, the product and the inbox at once, in the
company's own voice, and the post-incident report writes itself from what
happened. Beacon is used by support teams at growing SaaS companies who
would rather keep a customer than explain an outage twice.
