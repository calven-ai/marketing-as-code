# Three-tier measurement for events and programs

## The tiers

| Tier | What | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1, activity | registrations, badge scans, foot traffic, impressions, sends, opens | record it; never the success criterion, never what gets optimised |
| 2, outcomes | qualified conversations held, meetings booked during or after, opportunities created with the program as source, pipeline influenced (any program touch on the deal) | the scoreboard |
| 3, decisive | cost per qualified meeting, cost per opportunity, closed-won influenced; each ranked against the other channels' cost for the same outcome | the go or no-go for next time |

A retro that stops at tier 1 has measured effort, not results. Tier 2
needs the source or campaign field set on the CRM records
(`data/ontology/naming.md`), and tier 3 needs the spend in
`data/ads/snapshots/YYYY-MM-DD-finance-invoices.csv` or the brief's
budget line.

## Follow-up rules the retro checks

- Timing: within 24 to 48 hours of the conversation; every day of delay
  roughly halves response rates. A retro records the actual send date
  against this.
- Tiered by conversation quality: hot (a personal note with the agreed
  next step), warm (a relevant asset tied to the stated problem), cold
  scans (one light touch or nothing).
- Context captured during the event (what they said, what they care
  about, the agreed next step) is what makes the follow-up specific; no
  context, generic follow-up, no result.
- Pre-booked meetings (one to three weeks ahead) beat hoping for hallway
  conversations; count how many were pre-booked versus walk-up.

## Reading the numbers

- Judge a recurring event on a two-to-three-run trend; the first run
  underperforms its steady state.
- Small niche and regional events (50 to 200 people) often return more
  qualified leads per unit of spend than large conferences; compare on
  tier 3, not attendance.
- Rank the program against paid and outbound on cost per opportunity
  before deciding to repeat it.

## Retro questions

What did tier 2 and 3 say against the brief's goal; which sessions,
assets or people produced the conversations; how fast and how specific
was the follow-up; what did the pre-event promotion cost per registrant
and per attendee; what would change the tier-3 numbers most next time.
