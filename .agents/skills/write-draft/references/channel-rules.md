# Per-type structures

Condensed from the source above and rewritten for this repo. Voice comes
from `brand/voice.md`; these are shapes.

## Blog post

Headline under 60 characters with the outcome and the primary keyword;
introduction of 100 to 150 words that hooks (a question, a number, a
claim, a scene) and says what the post covers; three to five H2
sections, one idea each, with at least one number, example or quote per
section; conclusion of 75 to 100 words with one CTA; meta description
under 160 characters. Paragraphs of two to four sentences, a subheading
every 200 to 300 words, active voice, the key point first.

## Social

Hook, body, ask, tags. LinkedIn: human and professional, paragraph
breaks, the hook inside the first 1,300 characters before "see more",
stories and lessons. X: short and direct, the strongest words first,
threads for anything longer, reply to replies.

## Newsletter

Subject under 50 characters; preview that complements it; one line of
value under the header; two or three blocks with a bold first sentence;
one primary CTA, visually distinct; footer with unsubscribe and company
details. Personalise where the data is real.

## Landing page

Headline under ten words with the primary benefit; subheadline with the
context; hero with the CTA; three or four benefit sections; social
proof; objections as FAQ or trust signals; the CTA again at the bottom.
"You" language, benefits before features, every section answers "so
what", fewer form fields.

## Press release

Factual headline under 80 characters; optional subheadline; dateline;
lead paragraph with who, what, when, where, why; supporting paragraphs
with one or two attributed quotes; boilerplate; media contact.

## Case study

Title "[Customer] achieves [result] with [product]"; a snapshot box
(industry, size, product, key result); challenge; solution; results
with specific numbers; a customer quote; one CTA. The `case-study` skill
carries the quote-approval checklist.

## Keywords on the page

One primary keyword in the headline, first paragraph, one subheading, the
meta description and the slug; two or three secondaries in the body
where they read naturally; two or three internal links, one or two
external to sources; alt text on images. Write for the reader first.

## CTAs by context

Blog: "Read the full guide", "Subscribe". Landing: "Start free trial",
"Get a demo", "See pricing". Email: "Read the story", "Claim your spot",
"Reply". Social: "Comment", "Save this". Case study: "See how it works",
"Talk to us". One primary CTA per page or email; above the fold on a
landing page, after the value in an email, at the end of a post.
