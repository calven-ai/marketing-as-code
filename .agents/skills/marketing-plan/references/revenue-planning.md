# Bottom-up, top-down, and the gap

Condensed from the source above (a revenue-operations planning guide);
the method the marketing plan borrows for its targets.

## The principle

A plan sales owns and can defend beats a perfect plan nobody believes.
Three reads converge: capacity (what the team can produce), strategy
(what leadership wants), market (what is achievable). The plan of record
is the contract between them.

## Bottom-up, five steps

1. Split the target by revenue type: new business, expansion, renewal.
   Baseline each from the CRM snapshots.
2. Capacity: reps times ramp factor times quota, plus the inbound and
   expansion overlay. For marketing: the funnel volumes each stage can
   produce at last year's conversion rates.
3. Named pipeline: the deals already known, with dates, laid over the
   capacity number.
4. Growth assumptions per stream: renewals most predictable, expansion
   next, new business most variable.
5. An assumption sheet: every number with its source, an owner and a
   confidence (high, medium, low).

## Reconciliation

Finance or leadership builds the top-down number independently. In one
meeting: frame the gap, present bottom-up, present top-down, close the
gap with named scenarios that each have an owner and an assumption, lock
the plan of record. A stretch with no scenario is a wish disguised as a
plan.

Keep three versions: the original bottom-up, the stretch, and the plan of
record; reforecasts compare against both, so a miss traces to an
assumption, not to "execution".

## When to reforecast

On a fixed cadence (a light monthly review, a full quarterly one) or
when a trigger fires: pipeline coverage under 2.5 times, a two-month run
of forecast variance over 20 percent, NRR under 105, CAC payback over 14
months, a large churn, a hiring slip, a product delay of six weeks or
more. Quotas, headcount and compensation do not move mid-year; targets
and scenario owners can.

## Benchmarks the source cites

Pipeline coverage 3 to 3.5 times is healthy; under 2.5 is critical.
Forecast within 10 percent of actual is strong, within 20 normal, over
25 a broken process. Healthy quota attainment 70 to 80 percent of reps;
the 2025 median it cites was 46. External figures, for context.

## Ten questions before locking a plan

A planning calendar with dates; a bottom-up built without the top-down
in the room; three versions kept; every unit of stretch explained with
an owner; reforecasts compared to both baselines; locked parameters
written down; finance and operations reviewing changes together; named
reforecast triggers; the team knowing the plan of record; misses traced
to assumptions. Fewer than five yes answers: plan the planning first.
