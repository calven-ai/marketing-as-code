# Novelty rules: is this actually new?

Use these to judge each piece of new evidence against what was already known (the existing card, the last digest, the page as it was before). The question: does it change an operative, market-facing fact?

## Verdicts

- **new**: a fact or condition with no equivalent in the earlier evidence.
- **changed**: a price, limit, date, billing commitment, eligibility, scope, status, promise or condition differs from the earlier evidence. New wording that contradicts what the site still says elsewhere also counts as changed.
- **promoted**: an existing claim becomes the main headline, or gets a similarly large jump in prominence. Reordering body content is not promotion.
- **restated**: every material fact and condition was already communicated, directly or through an unambiguous equivalent calculation.

Only earlier evidence can establish a restatement. The new text locates the claim; it cannot prove the claim existed before. Not finding a match is not proof that it was never said.

Two look-alikes that are never restated:

- A new announcement banner, launch strip or homepage callout ("X is here", "Introducing X", "New: X") is **promoted**, even when the offering was described elsewhere: the offering is not news, but the company now leads with it. An ordinary heading or card on an inner page is judged by the verdicts above.
- A newly listed item in a list, directory, table or grid (an integration, plan, feature, partner) is **new** unless that same item appeared before. A name that only overlaps an existing entry is a different item: a "Helpdesk" entry added beside an existing "Helpdesk Analytics" is new.

A compound claim is restated only when every material part is supported. If one part changed, the whole is changed or new; name the part that changed. A matching price does not establish matching eligibility, billing commitment, refund terms, capacity, geography or status.

Historical, expired, superseded, hypothetical and demonstration text does not establish an operative offer. A promise in the company's own voice differs from text inside an example conversation or a customer story.

## Equivalent numbers

A number counts as already known if it follows exactly from earlier facts about the same subject, currency, unit, eligibility and billing basis. Only these calculations are allowed:

- annual total: the annual-billed monthly-equivalent price times 12
- monthly equivalent: the annual total divided by 12
- discounted: the ordinary price times (1 minus the stated discount as a fraction)
- undiscounted: the discounted price divided by (1 minus the stated discount as a fraction)
- discounted annual total: the ordinary monthly price times (1 minus the stated annual discount as a fraction) times 12

Use exact arithmetic; never invent rounding or a discount. Example with made-up numbers: an annual-billed rate of $50/mo implies $600/year, and a $62.50 monthly price with a 20% annual discount implies the same $600/year; $750/year follows from neither.

Arithmetic never creates a billing option: a monthly-only plan does not gain annual billing because its price can be multiplied by twelve. A discount stated for one plan or audience does not apply to another.

## Toggles and flattened widgets

A captured page can contain both sides of a monthly/annual toggle, repeat prices, reverse their order, or show a calculator in one state. Compare billing bases, not adjacent amounts. Work out which price is which from labels, table headers, paired plan prices and totals; order alone is not enough. A monthly equivalent shown next to its annual total ($50/mo and $600 a year) is annual billing. If you cannot establish which billing state is shown, keep the evidence and state the ambiguity, but do not report a price change only because a widget dropped its label. A capacity increment such as "+N" is not a price of N.

## Regional re-renders

A different currency symbol or the same price converted, different number or date formats, the same text in another language, a region-specific banner: not a change. A switched currency whose numbers no conversion explains is a regional price list, added beside the existing figures and labelled by currency. Zero is zero in every currency.

## Evidence and doubt

For restated, changed and promoted, quote the earlier wording verbatim, one contiguous passage including the subject and its labels. Never join separate passages. When unsure, keep the evidence as new or changed and say what is ambiguous: missing a real change costs more than repeating a known fact.
