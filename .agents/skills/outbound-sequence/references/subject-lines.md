# Subject lines

Condensed from the source above.

## Rules

- Two to four words, lowercase, no punctuation. Two-word subjects open
  about 60 percent more often than five-word ones; mobile truncates at 30
  to 35 characters.
- Look internal, as if from a colleague: "reply rates", "hiring ops",
  "Q2 forecast", "second page", "onboarding gap".
- Personalise with context, not with the name: `{{company_initiative}}`,
  `{{challenge}}`, `{{competitor}}`, `{{trigger}}`. A first name in the
  subject cuts replies by about 12 percent.
- Default to statements. A specific pain question ("need help with
  `{{challenge}}`?") can work; "quick question?" and "have 15 minutes?"
  do not.

## Patterns to avoid

| Pattern | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| sales words: increase, boost, ROI | fewer opens |
| urgency: ASAP, urgent, last chance | fewer opens, spam risk |
| "!!!" or "??" | far fewer opens |
| numbers and percentages | fewer opens |
| emojis | reads as consumer marketing |
| the product pitch in the subject | far fewer replies |
| empty subject | more opens, fewer replies |
| "free", "guarantee", "act now" | deliverability risk |

## Executives

They see 300 to 400 emails a day and decide in seconds. Understated wins:
`{{company_initiative}}`, "an update", "a question", a project name they
would recognise. Anything that smells of a pitch is deleted.
