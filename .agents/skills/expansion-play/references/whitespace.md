# Whitespace

Condensed from the source above. Whitespace is the gap between what an
account uses and what it could use, scored so the biggest gaps at the
healthiest accounts rise to the top.

## Five dimensions, each scored 0 to 100 on penetration

| Dimension | Question |
| --- | --- |
| Products | which of our products fit their use case, and which do they own |
| Seats or users | licences bought against the people at the account who could use them |
| Departments | teams using it against teams that could |
| Features | features adopted against premium capabilities that fit them |
| Stakeholders | contacts we know against the decision-makers, influencers and users we should |

Whitespace is 100 minus penetration, averaged across the dimensions you
can actually score; say which ones you could not.

## Run it

1. Map high-touch accounts fully and the top fifth of mid-touch accounts;
   low-touch accounts run on product signals only.
2. Score the five dimensions from the snapshots (seats and features from
   usage or billing, stakeholders from the CRM contact list, departments
   from user emails or titles, products from the subscription).
3. Average to a whitespace score.
4. Rank by whitespace times health times contract timing (a renewal
   inside 120 days scores higher than one a year out).
5. Present the top accounts as a growth conversation in the QBR, not as a
   quota.
6. Refresh quarterly.

## Conversion targets the source gives

| Segment | Mapped | To pipeline in six months | Close rate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| high-touch, above 50k ARR | all | 40 percent | 50 to 60 |
| mid-touch, 10 to 50k | top 20 percent | 25 | 40 to 50 |
| low-touch, under 10k | product signals | 10 | 30 to 40 |

External figures; the team's own rates come from comparing this quarter's
list with next quarter's pipeline snapshot.
