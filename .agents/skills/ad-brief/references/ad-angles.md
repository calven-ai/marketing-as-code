# Angles, limits and the iteration loop

Condensed from the ad-creative skill above.

## Eight angle families

| Angle | Shape | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pain | stop doing the painful thing | "Stop rebuilding the same report every Monday" |
| Outcome | reach the result in a stated time | "First campaign report in an hour, not a week" |
| Social proof | join the people who already did | "Used by 120 marketing teams" (with a source) |
| Curiosity | the thing others know | "The one file most marketing teams never version" |
| Comparison | unlike the alternative | "Unlike a wiki, it answers" |
| Urgency | a real deadline | only when the deadline is real |
| Identity | built for this role | "Built for marketing ops leads" |
| Contrarian | the common practice is wrong | "Your positioning doc is stale, and it looks fine" |

Three to five angles per campaign, then five or more headlines and two
or three descriptions per angle. Angles are different reasons to click;
variations are different words for the same reason. Test angles first.

## Platform limits

| Platform | Field | Limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Google RSA | headlines | 30 characters, up to 15; each must stand alone in any combination |
| Google RSA | descriptions | 90 characters, up to 4 |
| Google RSA | display paths | 2 x 15 characters |
| LinkedIn | intro text | 150 characters recommended, 600 max |
| LinkedIn | headline | 70 recommended, 200 max |
| LinkedIn | description | 100 recommended, 300 max |
| Meta | primary text | 125 visible, 2,200 max; hook first |
| Meta | headline, description | 40, 30 recommended |
| X | post, headline, description | 280, 70, 200 |

RSA sets need a keyword headline, a benefit headline and a CTA headline.
Use the space: a 12-character headline in a 30-character field wastes
half the slot.

## Copy standards

Specific numbers over vague claims; benefits over features; active
verbs; a description that adds proof or removes an objection ("no credit
card") instead of repeating the headline. Avoid jargon the persona would
not use, superlatives without a number, all caps, and promises the
landing page cannot keep.

## Grounding

Every concept cites its source: a review, a customer quote, a winning ad,
a number in `strategy/`. Keep an inputs set the team refreshes: the ten
to twenty best-performing ads of the last 90 days, fifty to a hundred
verbatim reviews or call quotes, and the comments on past ads (objections
and unprompted praise are untested angles). If that set is empty, say so
and ask for it before writing volume.

## Iterating on performance

1. Winners: what themes, structures, words and lengths the top variants
   share (from `data/ads/snapshots/`).
2. Losers: which angles fell flat and what they share.
3. New round: extend the winning angles, test one or two new ones, drop
   the losing patterns; one variable per round.
4. Log the round in the piece's `draft.md`: round, top performers,
   patterns, angles tested, angles retired.

Judge nothing under 1,000 impressions. Cycle formats (us versus them,
stat callout, review card, before and after, founder note) rather than
repeating a favourite.

## Retargeting variants

Never re-show the same offer. Write, per retargeting audience in the
campaign plan: an objection ad (the top reason people did not act, in
their words), a proof ad (testimonials and numbers behind the original
claim), an other-offer ad (a different entry point), and a value-first ad
(an audit, a template, a tool).
