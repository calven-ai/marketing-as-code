# Sequence structure: spacing, angles, the breakup

Condensed from the source above.

## The default cadence

| Touch | Day | Angle |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 0 | the personalised hook, the problem, a soft CTA |
| 2 | 3 | one new piece of value: a stat, an insight, a resource |
| 3 | 7 to 8 | social proof: a similar company's result |
| 4 | 14 | a new insight or trend |
| 5 | 21 to 28 | the breakup |

Three to five touches in all. Each stands alone (the reader may see only
one), adds exactly one new reason to reply, and never says "just checking
in", "bumping this", "did you see my last email" or "following up on my
previous message".

More than half of replies come from follow-ups, and the first follow-up
adds roughly half again to the reply rate; by the fourth follow-up
response drops sharply and complaints rise, so stop at five.

## Timing

Tuesday to Thursday, 9 to 11 or 13 to 15 in the recipient's time zone.
Avoid Monday mornings and Friday afternoons.

## The breakup

Acknowledge the attempts, allow that now is not the time, say it is the
last message, leave the door open, and honour it: no contact after the
breakup unless they reply.

Pattern: "I have not heard back, so I will assume now is not the time.
One thing before I close the loop: `{{one_sentence_insight}}`. If that
changes anything, reply; otherwise no hard feelings."

The 1-2-3 variant asks for a number: 1 interested, 2 not now (check back
in three months), 3 not interested (stop). Whatever they answer is
recorded and respected.

## CTA by seniority

Executives and founders: "Curious?", "Worth two minutes?". Managers: a
specific value statement plus a concrete step.
