# Body frameworks for a cold touch

Condensed from the source above. Pick one per step; rotate across the
sequence. Every example number in the source is illustrative; in this repo
a number in a touch must trace to `strategy/positioning.md` or a snapshot.

| Framework | Shape | Use when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| PAS | problem, agitate, solution, soft CTA | problem-aware, not solution-aware; the default |
| BAB | before, after, bridge | a clear transformation; emotional buyers |
| QVC | pain question, one line of value, direct CTA | executives who want brevity |
| AIDA | hook or stat, the specific challenge, proof, CTA | data-driven buyers, high-ticket |
| PPP | genuine praise, picture of better, gentle push | senior people, only with a real trigger |
| Star, story, solution | a customer, their challenge, the result | a strong case study exists |
| SCQ | situation, complication, question | consultative selling; mirrors an internal memo |
| ACCA | contrarian hook, plain explanation, proof, strong CTA | analytical buyers (engineers, CFOs, ops) |
| Mouse trap | one observation plus one binary question, 1 to 2 sentences | maximum brevity; curiosity replies |
| Observation, problem, proof, ask | notice, the challenge it creates, a result, a low-friction ask | most first touches |
| Trigger, insight, ask | a recent event, the challenge it typically creates, a similar win, ask | signal-led outreach |

## Constraints that apply to all of them

- One idea per touch, one CTA per touch.
- 25 to 75 words is the sweet spot for the first touch; never over 100.
- Lead with "you" and "your"; keep "I" and "we" to a minimum.
- Plain words at a reading level a tired person on a phone can follow.
- The personalisation must connect to the problem; if removing it does not
  break the email's logic, it is decoration.
- No feature lists, no HTML, no images, no multiple links, no fake "Re:".
- Cut "I hope this finds you well", "synergy", "leverage", "circle back",
  "best-in-class", "quick question".

## CTA types, from least to most friction

"Worth exploring?"; "Would this be useful?"; "Open to a look?"; a yes or no
question; a specific 15-minute slot. Executives get the lowest friction;
managers respond to a quantified outcome plus a concrete next step. Never
ask for 30 minutes in the first touch.
