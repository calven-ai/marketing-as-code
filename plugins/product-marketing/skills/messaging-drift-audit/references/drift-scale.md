# Drift and coverage scales

Open this in procedure steps 5 and 7.

## Drift severity, per surface

Judge framing and claims, not completeness. Give the highest severity the text supports.

| Severity | When |
| --- | --- |
| **high** | The dominant description materially misrepresents what the company is or does, or what the product is for: it files the company in a wrong, legacy or generic category instead of the current one, or its central claims contradict the reference. |
| **medium** | Framing or claims that need correcting but do not misrepresent the whole: emphasis that points buyers at an outdated or secondary angle, a superseded slogan or retired tagline, stale proof (an old customer count or statistic), or a bounded overclaim of a capability or scope. |
| **low** | On message and consistent with the current reference, just briefer or worded differently; or an accurate description of one product in that product's own category, with nothing stale or contradicted. Omission of detail belongs here. |
| **none** | Matches the reference. |

Limits on escalation:

- A superseded slogan, generic wording, or one isolated wrong claim alone is not high.
- A surface with no framing text (only a URL, a bare name or a page title) is never high.
- An accurate category for the product being described is not drift, but it does not cancel other drift on the same surface.
- The reference being silent on something does not make the surface outdated or contradicted.
- Do not assume an ambiguous description is company-wide; decide from the text whether it describes the company or one product.

Different company: mark a surface as a different company only when the text or URL clearly identifies another business (a different domain, a product line or industry the reference never mentions, a different founding or location). An inaccurate, outdated or generic description of the company is drift, not a different company. When unsure, it is the company. Still give a best-guess severity and say whose profile it is.

Assessment: one sentence, at most 12 words, naming the specific drift or confirming alignment. No preamble, no restating the surface name.

## Vertical coverage, per priority vertical

Structural checks first:

| Level | When |
| --- | --- |
| **None** | No variation or page targets the vertical. |
| **Placeholder** | A variation targets it, but its angle and lead are empty or still template text. |

Then judge specificity, not length or polish. A short line naming a real vertical pain beats a
long generic one.

| Level | When |
| --- | --- |
| **Generic** | Angle and lead are boilerplate that would read correctly for almost any industry (growth, trust, unlock potential). |
| **Partial** | Exactly one of angle or lead is specific to this vertical; the other is generic. |
| **Tailored** | Both angle and lead are specific to this vertical: they name its regulations, buyers, workflows or pains. |

Note: one sentence, at most 25 words, saying what would make the coverage stronger or confirming
it is well tailored. On a rerun, keep the earlier level unless the evidence clearly warrants a
different one; do not move it by a hair.
