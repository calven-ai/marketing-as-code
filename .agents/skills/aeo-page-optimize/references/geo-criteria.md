# Five criteria for one page, with weights

Condensed from AgriciDaniel/claude-seo `seo-geo` (May 2026 revision). The
study numbers are the source's; treat them as direction, not law, and
re-check before quoting them to a customer.

## What the studies say

- Brand mentions correlate with AI citations about three times more
  strongly than backlinks (Ahrefs, 75,000 brands): video mentions
  strongest, then forum and encyclopedia presence; domain rating weak.
- Only about 11 percent of domains are cited by both ChatGPT and Google
  AI Overviews for the same query; optimise per engine.
- About 44 percent of citations come from the first 30 percent of a page;
  front-load the citable passage.
- Content under three months old is about three times as likely to be
  cited; pages stale for six months lose eligibility. A refresh schedule
  is one of the highest-leverage plays.
- Pages with multi-modal elements are selected far more often.

## Scoring (100)

### 1. Citability, 25

Optimal passage length 134 to 167 words; direct answer in the first 40
to 60 words of a section; quotable sentences with specific facts;
self-contained blocks; claims attributed to a source; definitions in the
"X is" form; data points not found elsewhere. Weak: vague statements,
opinion without evidence, buried conclusions.

### 2. Structural readability, 20

Clean H1 to H2 to H3; question-form headings; two-to-four-sentence
paragraphs; tables for comparisons; lists for steps; a clear FAQ block.
Weak: walls of text, inconsistent hierarchy, information buried in prose.

### 3. Multi-modal, 15

Relevant images, embedded or linked video, charts, interactive tools,
structured data describing the media.

### 4. Authority and brand signals, 20

Author byline with credentials; published and updated dates; citations to
primary sources; organisation credentials; expert quotes; entity presence
(encyclopedia, knowledge graph, forums, video, professional networks).
Weak: anonymous, undated, unsourced, absent elsewhere.

### 5. Technical accessibility, 20

AI crawlers do not execute JavaScript: server-side rendering for the
main content; crawler access in `robots.txt`; `llms.txt` presence noted
but given no ranking weight (Google states it neither helps nor hurts).

## Quick wins

Definition in the first 60 words; 134-to-167-word answer blocks;
question headings; sourced statistics; visible dates; Person schema for
authors; allow the key crawlers.

## Medium effort

Author bio with credentials and profile links; server-side rendering for
key pages; presence on the forums and video channels the category reads;
comparison tables; a real FAQ section.

## High impact

Original research or a survey; an encyclopedia entry where the brand
qualifies; a video channel; entity linking (`sameAs`) across platforms;
a unique tool or calculator.
