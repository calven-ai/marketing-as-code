# Finding rules

Open this file at step 4. Every market finding has these fields; leave a field empty (never a guess, never filler text) when the report does not support it.

| Field | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Type | Prediction, market sizing, trend or recommendation. Nothing else |
| Summary | One concise line naming the finding. Required, never empty |
| Subject | The market or segment the finding is about (for example "incident communication tools", "EU mid-market support software"). Empty when the report covers one market and the subject is implicit |
| Horizon | Predictions only: the target year the analyst names, for example 2028 |
| Value and unit | Market sizing only: the number as a number, and its unit (USD bn, %, seats). Add the year it refers to and the market definition the report uses, since figures from different reports are rarely comparable |
| Excerpt | A short verbatim quote from the report that supports the finding |
| Page | The page number, or the section heading for notes without pages |

## Telling the types apart

- A forecast with a year ("By 2028, most support teams will...") is a prediction, even when it reads like a trend.
- A number about the size, spend or growth of a market is market sizing; a percentage of buyers doing something is a prediction or a trend, depending on whether it looks forward.
- A recommendation must teach something about the market: where to invest, which capabilities matter, how to respond to a shift in buyer needs. Advice about the firm's own services, or generic buying method, is not a finding.

## Not market findings (keep out of this section)

- Vendor placements, tiers, quadrants, scores and per-vendor strengths or cautions: these go in the Vendors section.
- Process boilerplate about using the analyst firm (inquiry calls, webinars, companion notes, "use this as one input").
- Generic procurement method (requirements, proof of concept, weighted scoring, stakeholder involvement).
