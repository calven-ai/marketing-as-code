# Comparison page formats

Open this at step 3 to pick the format, and again at step 6 for its section order. Pick by the keyword's intent, not by preference.

| Format | URL pattern | Intent | Keywords |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Singular alternative | `/alternatives/<them>` | Actively switching from one tool | "<them> alternative", "alternative to <them>" |
| Plural alternatives | `/alternatives/<them>-alternatives` | Researching options | "<them> alternatives", "best <them> alternatives" |
| Us vs them | `/vs/<them>` | Head-to-head comparison | "<you> vs <them>", "<them> vs <you>" |
| Them vs them | `/compare/<them>-vs-<other>` | Comparing two rivals; you are the informed third option | "<them> vs <other>" |

## Section order per format

- **Singular alternative:** validate the pain that makes people switch; your position; detailed comparison; who should switch and who should stay; migration; proof from switchers; call to action.
- **Plural alternatives:** the pains that send people looking; the evaluation criteria; four to seven genuine alternatives, you first but real competition, each with who it fits; a comparison table; recommendations by use case.
- **Us vs them:** short verdict; comparison table; one section per attribute; honest "who it's for" for both; proof; migration support.
- **Them vs them:** both products fairly; attribute comparisons; who each serves; your position as the alternative; a three-way table.

## Sections every page carries

A two or three sentence summary of the key differences; a comparison table; paragraphs that say when each difference matters, not only that it exists; pricing by tier with total cost where both publish it; "who it's for" for every option; migration (what transfers, what needs setting up, a switcher's words); proof from people who switched.

## Research before writing

Read their product pages and docs; verify current pricing and hidden costs (minimums, add-ons, annual-only tiers); read reviews on the major software review sites; read their own comparison pages about you; ask the person for switcher stories in both directions. Keep one profile per competitor as the single source (the battlecard), refreshing pricing and major features quarterly and immediately when a customer flags a change.

## Search and AI answers

Plural alternatives pages earn citations in AI answers, but rarely a recommendation unless reviews, forums and analysts agree elsewhere; an emerging brand may see its competitors surface through its own page. Publish anyway: the page frames the intent. Link comparison pages to each other and to a hub page, and answer the common questions in a FAQ.
