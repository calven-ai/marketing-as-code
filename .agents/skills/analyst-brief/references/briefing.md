# Analyst briefings: structure, preparation, questions, cycles

Condensed from the source above.

## Briefing versus inquiry

| | Briefing | Inquiry |
| --- | --- | --- |
| direction | you present | you ask |
| when | a launch, a major announcement | strategy validation, market intelligence |
| cost | free | subscription |

Other interactions: advisory (deep consultation, premium), commissioned
research (paid study), speaking at analyst events (registration fee).

## The 30 minutes

| Minutes | Content |
| --- | --- |
| 1 to 5 | the market problem, why now, the different approach |
| 5 to 15 | the solution, named customers where permitted, quantified outcomes, roadmap priorities |
| 15 to 25 | their questions, your questions, market dynamics, positioning feedback |
| 25 to 30 | announcements to flag, research participation, follow-ups |

At least half the time is discussion. Lead with the customer's problem,
use specific metrics, acknowledge gaps, ask what trends they see, listen
more than you present.

## Preparation checklist

Their recent coverage; their evaluation methodology; substantive questions
ready; the presentation time-boxed; customer examples with numbers.

## Language

Good: "We see customers struggle with X; here is how we approach it and
the results." "What trends should we be aware of?" "We are strong here and
still developing there; the roadmap addresses it in Q3." "How are you
segmenting the market for the next report?"

Bad: a 45-minute deck with no discussion; "the industry leader" without
evidence; refusing to discuss roadmap limits; "we have no competitors";
reading slides.

## Inquiry questions worth the subscription

How to position against a named competitor in a segment; messaging gaps
for a persona; objections they hear about vendors in the space; how the
market thinks about an emerging trend. Not: "will you include us",
"what are competitors doing", "will you be a reference".

## Report cycles

| Phase | When | Do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| pre-cycle | 6 months or more before | regular briefings, relationship depth |
| RFI | before the deadline | answer the question asked, with evidence and named references (with permission), gaps acknowledged, on time |
| demo or briefing | scheduled | prepared, rehearsed |
| draft review | a short window | factual corrections only |
| publication | release day | amplification plan, a prepared response |

Positioning categories and their strategy: leader (keep momentum);
challenger (invest in the innovation narrative); visionary (proof
points); niche (own it, expand deliberately).

## Tiers and cadence

Tier 1, 6 to 10 analysts, quarterly briefings and inquiry access; tier 2,
10 to 15, semi-annual plus major news; tier 3, a watchlist engaged
yearly. Quick updates only for major news; thought leadership shared
monthly; an event or in-person meeting yearly; customer introductions
when relevant.

## What sales and AR trade

AR gives sales report summaries and talking points, analyst quotes for
proposals, positioning guidance, alerts on negative coverage. Sales gives
AR competitive intelligence from deals, reference candidates, a read on
which analysts influence buyers, and win or loss context.

## Anti-patterns

Briefing everyone; assuming payment buys coverage; hostility to
disagreement; one briefing before a report; dismissing negative feedback;
exaggeration; treating an inquiry as a sales call; disappearing after
publication.
