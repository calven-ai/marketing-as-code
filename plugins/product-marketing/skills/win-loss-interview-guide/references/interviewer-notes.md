# Interviewer notes

Open this file at step 6, and attach it to a live interview guide so the interviewer has it on the call.

## Who and when

Someone who was not on the deal, ideally a few weeks after the decision, while the buyer still remembers the details. The buyer is doing the company a favour, and their candour is the whole value of the call.

## The shape of a turn

- A short neutral acknowledgement ("Got it.", "Okay.", "Thanks.", or nothing), then one question. Under about 30 words.
- No verdict on the answer ("great", "interesting", "that makes sense"), no summary of it.
- Criticism of the company and praise of a rival get the same neutral acknowledgement. Never defend, excuse, agree or soften. Harsh feedback is what the call is for: ask for the specifics.
- Quote at most a few of their words, and only to aim a follow-up: "You said an integration was missing. Which one?"
- At a new section, one short clause is enough: "Now the sales team."

## Follow-ups

People say more out loud than they type. An answer that names nothing is a failed answer, however complete it sounds. Follow up once, with one short question, when the answer:

| The answer... | Example | Follow-up |
|---|---|---|
| names a category instead of a thing | "They were missing one integration we really needed." | "Which integration was that?" |
| compares without a size | "It was actually cheaper." | "Roughly how much cheaper, if you're able to say?" |
| judges without a reason | "They didn't really understand support teams." | "What did they miss about it?" |
| mentions in passing something that moved the decision | "There was pressure to go the other way." | "Where was that pressure coming from?" |
| covers only part of the question | Answers the better side of the comparison only | Ask for the other side. |

No follow-up when the answer already names the thing, gives a number or tells a specific story. One follow-up per answer, then take whatever comes. Two follow-ups in a row that add nothing mean the buyer is done: keep the rest brisk.

## Ratings

Say the scale and what the ends mean the first time ("1 to 4, where 1 is poor and 4 is excellent"); after that, "on that same scale". A number or words both count; never press for the number. If they give no reason, ask one follow-up worded for the rating given: at the top, "What earned that?"; below the top, "What would have raised it?". Never ask what kept it from a level they just gave.

## Handling the call

- If an answer already covered a later question, do not re-ask it cold: name what you have in a few words and ask only for what is missing.
- "I don't know", "I'd rather not say" or "next question" ends that question: "No problem," and move on.
- Money, headcount and contract terms: ask with the way out in the same sentence; accept a decline at once.
- Never ask how a vendor name is spelled; names are cleaned up afterwards.
- Promise nothing: no discount, no roadmap item, no follow-up action.
- Close with two statements: thanks, and one specific thing they said that was useful. No question, no offer.

## Recording

Write answers in the buyer's words as they speak, not your summary. Mark a question "not answered" rather than fill it in. Note anything the guide had no question for at the end: it is often the most useful line of the call.
