# Checking assets

Open this in steps 10 and 11: choosing which assets to check, and judging each one.

## Choosing assets

You are not yet deciding whether an asset is wrong; you are deciding which are worth reading. An asset is a candidate when it describes the capability, plan, price, packaging or positioning the change touches; when it names the affected product and says what it does; or when it is where a reader would go for the fact the change makes true or untrue. It is not a candidate when it covers a different product line, people or process, or a subject the change has no bearing on.

Order candidates most likely affected first, at most ten per change. Judge each change on its own: an asset that fits one change is not thereby a candidate for another.

## Verdicts

For each asset and change, given that the change is now true, does the asset still hold? Read it as a reader would. Choose one:

- **No impact:** the change has no bearing on what this asset says, even if both concern the same product.
- **Needs review:** the asset is now incomplete or out of date: it covers the area that moved without stating the new fact, or implies something the change weakens.
- **Contradiction:** the asset states something the change makes untrue.
- **Already updated:** the asset already describes the new state. Saying so is as useful as flagging a problem.

Prefer the weaker verdict when torn. Silence is never a Contradiction; at most it is Needs review.

## The quote

Copy the smallest span of the asset that carries the point (the sentence that contradicts, the sentence that would need to change, or the one that already says it) character for character, with markdown stripped. Never quote the change in this column, and never rewrite. An empty quote is acceptable only for No impact.

## What it should say

Two or three sentences at most: what in the asset is affected and what it needs to say instead, using only facts from the change. Do not draft new marketing copy beyond that.

## Confidence

How sure you are of the verdict, not how important the change is. Use High only when you can point at a specific sentence. When an asset was cut off or only partly readable, judge what you saw and say the rest was not read.
