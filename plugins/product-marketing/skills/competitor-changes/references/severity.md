# Severity, confidence and corroboration

## Severity: how loudly a signal arrives

Rate severity only after deciding a signal is in the digest. Severity sorts the digest; it never decides what gets in. Never drop a signal for being "barely worth reporting", and never let doubt about inclusion pull every rating down.

Rate by what changed in the world, not by how sweeping the prose sounds, so the same grade means the same thing every week. The test: if a seller walked into a call tomorrow not knowing this, how badly would it go?

- **High**: they would be caught out. A price, a buyer, a competitor, or the company itself changed.
- **Medium**: they would sound out of date. The story changed; the facts did not.
- **Low**: it would not come up. The same story, arranged differently.

If you sell several products, rate a signal about one of them by what changed for that product's deals: a price change on one product is still High for the people selling it.

| Shape of the change | Severity | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Announces a large funding round | High | changes trajectory and credibility |
| Acquires another company, or is acquired | High | the company changed |
| Introduces a new pricing mechanic (per seat to consumption) | High | how they charge changed |
| Retires a plan tier | High | deals priced against it are affected |
| Broadens or narrows the stated target customer | High | who they sell to changed |
| Publishes a head-to-head comparison naming a competitor | High | a battlecard needs updating today |
| Removes a competitor comparison it used to publish | High | they stopped naming someone, equally telling |
| Adds a new named product to the portfolio, or kills one | High | what they sell changed |
| Enters a new region, country or market segment | High | who they can sell to changed |
| Changes CEO, or a founder departs | High | strategy follows |
| Earns a certification that unlocks a segment (FedRAMP, HIPAA) | High | deals closed to them are open |
| Repositions around a new narrative, same products | Medium | the story moved, nothing they sell did |
| Adds AI framing to an existing category claim | Medium | a story, not a move |
| Introduces a capability inside an existing product | Medium | a feature, not a product |
| Adds a marquee customer or a quantified outcome | Medium | strong proof, but proof |
| Earns an analyst placement | Medium | credibility, not a change in what they sell |
| Signs a named partnership or joins a platform ecosystem | Medium | reach, not product |
| Changes a VP or functional leader | Medium | signals intent, changes nothing yet |
| Earns a general security certification (SOC 2, ISO 27001) | Medium | table stakes met |
| Adds a subprocessor or vendor to a policy | Medium | a tell about what they are building |
| Restates an existing capability on a second page | Low | the same fact, a new page |
| Adds detail to a move already reported | Low | the news was the move |
| Changes a retention period or boilerplate clause with no new data category | Low | policy hygiene |
| A single customer win, a small proof point | Low | real but minor |

Read the pattern, not the words. The line runs between facts a deal turns on and the way a company talks about itself: a repositioning can sound enormous and be Medium; one changed number on a pricing page can sound trivial and be High.

- Removals are as strong as additions: a dropped comparison, a retired tier, an abandoned category claim are High-grade.
- Sweeping language is not severity: "reimagines", "redefines" and "elevates" describe every quarter's edits.
- Most competitor-years hold only a handful of High signals. Rating most of a week High means you are reading prose intensity. Do not force a spread either: a week with no Low is ordinary.
- When torn between two levels, pick the lower.

## Confidence: how well the evidence supports the claim

Confidence is independent of severity: a small change you can quote exactly is Low severity at High confidence.

From the competitor's own site:

- **High**: quotes from full page content that state the change outright (a price on the pricing page, a named partner in a release, a certification on the trust page).
- **Medium**: the change is there but the evidence is partial (one thin quote, a claim assembled across two pages, wording that implies the move without saying it). A signal resting only on a newly published page is capped at Medium.
- **Low**: you are reading intent into a fragment (a headline, a nav label, one line of a truncated page).

## Corroborating news

A news story is a third party's claim. Check it against a second independent source, the competitor's own site or newsroom, or a primary source (a filing, an official announcement).

- Confirmed by the competitor itself (its site, newsroom, or a press release it issued, judged by the issuer named in the boilerplate or media contact), or by two or more independent credible outlets: **High**.
- One credible outlet, no contradiction found: **Medium**.
- A single thin source, rumour-grade, or contradicted: **reject**. Do not include it at Low. A wrong signal is worse than a missing one.

A press release issued by a partner, customer or investor counts as one outlet, not as the competitor's confirmation. Check the story is about this competitor and not a same-name company, and that it is a recent move, not old news resurfacing (in a baseline run, that it happened in the window and still stands).
