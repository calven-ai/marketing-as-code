# Question bank and leading tests

Open this file at step 2 to build the set and at step 5 to check it. `<Company>` is the user's company; rating scales run 1 (worst) to 4 (best) unless the user uses another.

## Answer shapes

| Shape | How the answer is recorded |
|---|---|
| Open text | The buyer's words. |
| Yes or no | Yes or no, with a sentence of context welcome. |
| One option | Exactly one of the listed options; "other" in their words only if allowed. |
| Several options | Any of the listed options; "other" only if allowed. |
| Vendor list | Vendor names as the buyer says them; "nobody else" is a valid answer. |
| Rating | A whole number on the stated scale, both ends named, plus an optional comment. A rating in words maps to the nearest point. |
| Comparison | One row per vendor named: what this vendor did better than `<Company>`, what it did worse. An empty side stays empty. |

## Win version

**Competition**
1. Besides `<Company>`, which vendors did you evaluate? (Vendor list, "nobody else" allowed.)
2. How did the vendors you evaluated compare to `<Company>`? (Comparison, one vendor at a time. SKIP if question 1 was "nobody else".)

**Product**
3. Which of `<Company>`'s products did you evaluate or consider? (Several options. Only if there is more than one product.)
4. Which `<Company>` features did you like the most? (Open text, required.)
5. Which `<Company>` features were missing or fell short? (Open text, required.)

**Sales team**
6. How would you rate your experience with `<Company>`'s sales team? (Rating with comment.)
7. How would you rate the technical expertise of `<Company>`'s team? (Rating with comment.)
8. How would you rate the industry expertise of `<Company>`'s team? (Rating with comment.)
9. Overall, how would you rate your experience with `<Company>`'s sales process? (Rating with comment.)

**Pricing and legal**
10. How did `<Company>`'s pricing compare to the strongest alternative you evaluated? (One option: `<Company>` was less expensive; Priced similarly; `<Company>` was more expensive. SKIP if question 1 was "nobody else".)
11. Any feedback on `<Company>`'s pricing or licensing structure? (Open text, optional.)
12. How smooth was the contract negotiation process? (Rating with comment.)

**Closing**
13. In short, why did you pick `<Company>`? (Open text, required.)

## Loss version

**Competition**
1. Besides `<Company>`, which vendors did you evaluate? (Vendor list, "nobody else" allowed.)
2. Which of those vendors did you ultimately choose? (One vendor from question 1, or none.)
3. You mentioned no purchase was made. What led to that decision? (Several options: We built or kept our own solution; Budget was cut or reallocated; We couldn't prove the ROI to key stakeholders; Priorities shifted and the project was postponed; No solution fit our needs well enough; other allowed. ONLY if question 2 was none.)
4. How did those vendors compare to `<Company>`? (Comparison, one vendor at a time. Optional when question 2 was none, because a buyer who bought nothing is the likeliest to drop out here, and their comparison is still unbiased intelligence.)

**Product**: questions 3 to 5 of the win version, word for word. The same wording on both versions is what lets wins and losses be compared.

**Sales team**: questions 6 to 9 of the win version.

**Pricing and legal**
5. How did `<Company>`'s pricing compare to the vendor you selected? (Same three options. SKIP if question 2 was none.)
6. Any feedback on `<Company>`'s pricing or licensing structure? (Open text, optional.)

**Closing**
7. In short, what were the main reasons you did not select `<Company>`? (Open text, required.)

## Live interview additions

- Opening spine: "Walk me through how the decision was made, from the moment you started looking." Let them talk; map what they cover onto the questions above and do not re-ask it cold.
- Before the closing question: "Who was involved in the decision, and who had the final say?"
- Last: "What would have had to be true for this to go the other way?"

## Leading tests (every question must meet them)

- **No outcome or verdict in the wording.** Not "why did you reject us", not "what did we do wrong", not "what did you love".
- **No rival in the product questions.** "What was missing compared to Northstar?" leaves a buyer who bought nothing with no way to answer, and turns a gap into a comparison. The comparison question is where rivals go.
- **Likes and gaps are separate, both required, equal weight.**
- **Same product wording on win and loss.** A split wording makes the two impossible to count together.
- **No "would you agree", "wasn't it", "how much did X help".** Each presumes the answer.
- **One question at a time.** "How was the demo and the pricing?" gets half an answer.
- **No menu of reasons offered up front.** Ask open; options are for recording.
- **The product question asks what they evaluated, not what impressed them.**
- **Scales name both ends.** "1 to 4" alone leaves them guessing which end is good.
