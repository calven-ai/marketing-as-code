# Persona review checklist

Judge each criterion across the whole set. Partial is right when only some personas satisfy it.

## Section criteria (weight in brackets)

| Section | Criterion | Met when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Company objectives (1) | Objectives linked | Each persona links to concrete company objectives and says how its work moves them, not placeholders |
| Goals & KPIs (2) | KPIs have targets | Two to four KPIs, each with a metric and a target or direction; every target cited or labelled as an archetype |
| Pains (2) | Pains specific, impact-rated | Specific problems in the buyer's world, each rated High, Medium or Low, never the vendor's answer |
| Gains (1) | Gains tied to outcomes | Each gain ties to a business or personal outcome |
| Jobs to be done (1) | Jobs typed | Each job named and typed Functional, Social or Emotional |
| Objections (1) | Objections answered | Each objection is a real doubt in the buyer's words, paired with a grounded response |
| Messaging hooks (1) | Hooks grounded | Each hook is a specific line tied to a named pain or gain |
| Watering holes (1) | Channels named | Named communities, newsletters, events, channels; never "industry events" or "social media" |

## Set-level criteria (together weigh 5 against the sections' 10, so about a third of the score; equal weight each)

| Criterion | Met when |
| --- | --- |
| Covers the ICP's segments | Personas map to the segments the ICP names, with a sensible mix of buyers, stakeholders and users |
| Buying group complete | Economic buyer, champion or sponsor, technical evaluator, primary end users, plus the security, procurement or finance gates the deal really hits (typed Stakeholder). Each states its deal role: signs, sponsors, validates or can block. No conflicts |
| Distinct | Each persona is a distinct deal motion, differentiated by what only it owns; no two share most of their pains or hooks; none is another restated at a different seniority |
| Source diversity | Mostly vendor-neutral sources (analyst, practitioner, community, competitor framings); no single source, especially the company's own pages, dominates |
| Buyer voice | Pains and objections in the buyer's own language, usable by sales tomorrow; KPIs are the few the role tracks obsessively |
| Claims cited | Every market or role claim carries a link or file citation; uncited assertions are flagged |
| Complete | Four to six personas, each canvas substantially filled, none skeletal |

## Grading rules

- Template text, italic placeholders, "e.g." examples and "not enough data" markers are unmet. An untouched template scores near 0.
- The team's own words count as content, cited or not.
- Content labelled as inferred, or hedged, is partial at most.
- Set-level checks need content to check: consistency criteria are unmet while the sections they reason over are empty.
- A dated note saying the team confirmed they do not have the data ("Not tracked yet: no closed deals; confirmed 2026-10-01") is still unmet, but do not ask about it again within three months.

## Stereotype and vendor-echo patterns

| Pattern | Example | Why it hurts |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Irrelevant demographics | "42, two kids, loves trail running" | Adds nothing to how they buy; invites bias |
| Generic traits | "Busy, wants ROI, values efficiency" | True of everyone, so it guides nothing |
| Seniority restatement | "Support Manager" and "Senior Support Manager" with the same pains | One deal motion counted twice |
| Vendor echo | Pain: "No unified incident communication tool" | The product's pitch, not the buyer's problem |
| Strawman objection | "It's too good to be true" | Prepares sales for nothing |
| Invented precision | "Spends 37 percent of the week on incidents" with no source | Reads as research, is a guess |
