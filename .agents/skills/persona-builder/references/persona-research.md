# Persona research: extraction, synthesis, confidence

Condensed from the customer research skill in marketingskills. The three
modes are kept because they map onto what this repo holds: transcripts and
CRM snapshots (mode 1), review and community snapshots (mode 2), and
interviews the team still has to run (mode 3).

## Three research modes

1. Analyse what exists: transcripts, surveys, support tickets, win/loss
   notes, NPS verbatims.
2. Mine public signal: review sites, forums, community threads, job posts.
   Nobody was prompted, so the language is honest.
3. Primary research: interviews and surveys when the first two are thin.
   Thirty minutes per call, a small incentive, five-why laddering; the
   product-market-fit survey benchmark is 40 percent "very disappointed"
   if the product went away.

## Five dimensions to extract from every source

| Dimension | What to capture |
| --- | --- |
| Jobs to be done | Functional, emotional and social outcomes sought |
| Pain points | Unprompted frustrations; emotional language marks priority |
| Trigger events | What changed: new leader, missed target, team growth, competitor |
| Desired outcomes | Success in the customer's words and numbers |
| Language | Exact phrases ("drowning in spreadsheets", not "manual inefficiency") |

Per quote, record the source (path or URL), the verbatim text, the
context, sentiment, a theme tag (pain, trigger, outcome, alternative,
language) and what it says about the customer's profile.

## Synthesis

- Cluster themes across sources; score by frequency times intensity.
- Segment by company size, role, use case and tenure before averaging
  anything.
- Keep five to ten money quotes per theme.
- Flag contradictions between what customers say and what they do.

## Confidence labels

| Level | Criteria |
| --- | --- |
| High | Three or more independent sources, unprompted, consistent across segments |
| Medium | Two sources, or consistent within one segment only |
| Low | One source; possible outlier |

Guardrails: weight the last twelve months; note sample bias (reviews skew
to power users, tickets to problems, forums to technical people); require
five data points per segment before a persona is more than a draft.

## Where each audience talks

B2B SaaS: Reddit, G2, Hacker News, LinkedIn, Indie Hackers. SMB founders:
r/entrepreneur, r/smallbusiness, Product Hunt, Facebook groups.
Developers: r/devops, Stack Overflow, Discord. Enterprise: LinkedIn,
analyst reports, G2 enterprise filter, job postings. Early-stage fallback
when your own reviews do not exist: direct competitors' reviews, then
comparable products, then adjacent brands.

## Persona structure

Profile (title, company size, industry, reporting line, team); primary job
to be done; trigger events; top three pains; desired outcomes; objections
and fears; alternatives considered; key vocabulary with sources; how to
reach them (channels, content, people they follow).

## Anti-patterns

Cute names; one persona averaged across segments; details nobody said;
personas written once and never revisited (quarterly is the cadence).

## Gate questions before you start

What is the goal (messaging, persona, product gap, churn)? What assets
exist? Which segment? Which deliverable: synthesis report, quote bank,
persona, jobs map, competitive sentiment, or a gap analysis of what is
still unknown and how to learn it?
