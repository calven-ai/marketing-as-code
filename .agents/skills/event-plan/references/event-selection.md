# Choosing the role and the format

Condensed from the source above.

## Four roles

| Role | What it is | Model to use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| host | own the audience end to end: webinar, workshop, dinner, meetup, summit | `webinar-funnel.md` |
| sponsor or exhibit | buy access to someone else's audience | `sponsorship-roi.md` |
| speak | trade expertise for stage time and a recording | `speaking.md` |
| attend | no booth, no stage; engineer the meetings anyway | below |

## Hosting formats

| Format | Best for | Effort |
| --- | --- | --- |
| webinar | education and leads at scale | low to mid |
| workshop | product-qualified leads, activation | mid |
| dinner or small gathering | executive relationships, ABM | mid |
| meetup series | local community, recurring presence | mid |
| virtual summit | list building with partners | high |
| user conference | retention, expansion, authority | very high |

Two rules: the topic is the targeting (pick one the buyer would attend
even if they never bought), and recurring beats one-off.

## The attend motion

15 to 30 named people from the attendee and speaker lists; meetings
pre-booked one to three weeks out in 15-minute anchored slots; a side
dinner for 8 to 12 accounts if the budget allows; the same 24-to-48-hour
follow-up as everyone else.

## The 20/80 arc

Twenty percent of the value is at the event; eighty is before and after.
Before: the target list, pre-booked meetings ("a meeting booked two weeks
out is worth ten hopeful hallway collisions"), a reason to find you.
During: qualified conversations over raw contacts; capture context after
each one (what they said, what they care about, the agreed next step).
After: 24 to 48 hours, referencing what was actually discussed; tiered
follow-up; event source tags in the CRM.

## Measurement tiers

| Tier | Examples | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| vanity | registrations, badge scans, impressions | track, never optimise |
| real | qualified conversations, meetings, opportunities, pipeline influenced | the scoreboard |
| decisive | cost per qualified meeting, cost per opportunity, closed-won influenced | go or no-go |

Judge a recurring programme on a two-to-three-event trend, not one run.

## Failure modes

Sponsoring for awareness with no meeting target; follow-up two weeks late;
a weak topic that reminders cannot save; one-off budgeting (plan event
three before running event one); no recording; badge scans counted as
leads; writing off events after one bad conference; chasing the
mega-conference over the niche regional one.
