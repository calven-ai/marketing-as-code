# Designing the ask

Condensed from the source above (a referral and affiliate guide) and
narrowed to what a B2B reviews-and-references program uses.

## The loop

Trigger moment, share action, converted referral, reward, repeat. Every ask
in the program sits at a trigger moment; an ask sent on a calendar date
instead of a trigger is the most common reason a program stalls.

## Trigger moments, ranked

1. Right after the first measurable outcome (the "aha" moment).
2. After a milestone: a launch, a quarter of usage, a QBR with good numbers.
3. After exceptional support: a resolved escalation the customer praised.
4. After a renewal or an upgrade.

Never at signup, and never inside a renewal negotiation.

## Mechanism, ranked by conversion

In-product prompt, then a personal link, then an email from the account
owner, then social sharing, then a code. For B2B references the personal
email from the CSM wins; for reviews an in-product prompt plus a personal
follow-up.

## Incentives

- Single-sided (the customer only) is enough for high-value products and
  simpler to run; double-sided lifts conversion for self-serve.
- Lead with the larger-looking number, keep the reward tied to the trigger,
  and make the share one click with the message pre-written.
- B2B SaaS range in the source: 50 to 500 dollars, or one to three months
  free; enterprise is custom. Review sites often run their own gift-card
  programs; check the platform's rules before adding a second incentive.

## Diagnosing a weak program

| Symptom | Fix |
| --- | --- |
| Few asks answered | better trigger moments, simpler share, stronger reward |
| Referrals arrive but do not convert | the landing experience or the new-customer offer |
| Nobody knows the program exists | in-app prompt, a line in lifecycle emails |
| One-time referrers | tiered rewards, recognition |
| Abuse | caps, verification, terms |

## Nurture cadence after the first ask

Day 7 reminder, day 30 "know anyone who would benefit", day 60 a success
story with the ask again, and a prompt after each milestone.

## Measures

Active advocates in the last 30 days, ask-to-review conversion, share of
new customers from referrals, referral CAC against other channels, and
retention of referred customers. The source cites referred customers at
16 to 25 percent higher lifetime value and 18 to 37 percent lower churn;
treat those as external benchmarks, not this team's numbers.

## Launch checklist

Goal and metric, incentive, tracking and attribution, fraud rule, terms,
a tested end-to-end flow, an announcement to existing customers, a briefed
support team, and a review of the funnel after 30 days.
