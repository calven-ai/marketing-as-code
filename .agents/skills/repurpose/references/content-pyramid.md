# The content pyramid

How one piece becomes many without becoming a paste. Written for this
repo from common practice.

## Three tiers

| Tier | Shape | Effort | Count from one source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Pillar | long-form: blog, webinar, podcast, report | high | 1 |
| Derivative | medium: newsletter section, LinkedIn article, email sequence, short video | medium | 3 to 5 |
| Micro | short: posts, quotes, carousel, clips, community replies | low | 10 to 20 |

A good pillar yields fifteen to twenty-five pieces over a few weeks. The
calendar decides how many the team can actually ship.

## Extract before you write

From the source, in this order, into a scratch list:

1. Thesis: the single idea, one sentence.
2. Key points: five to ten, each a claim that could open a post.
3. Stories and examples: the concrete moments.
4. Numbers: each with its snapshot path or source line from the brief.
5. Quotes: lines worth repeating, with who said them and whether they
   are approved for reuse.
6. Actions: what a reader can do tomorrow.

Every variant is built from this list, never from re-reading the
source, so nothing drifts.

## Recipes

- Blog to thread: thesis as the first post, five to ten points one per
  post, a story or take of your own, a closing ask; ten to twelve posts
  at most; each stands alone in a feed.
- Blog to LinkedIn carousel: seven to ten takeaways, a cover slide, one
  takeaway per slide, a CTA slide with the source link; eight to twelve
  slides, three lines each.
- Blog to newsletter section: two sentences of personal intro, the
  three most useful points as headline plus a line, your own comment,
  the link.
- Blog to short video script: hook (0 to 5 s), context (5 to 15 s),
  three points (15 to 60 s), CTA (60 to 70 s); open with the most
  surprising point.
- Talk or podcast to blog: the thesis, five to eight segments, real
  quotes, a how-to or list structure, transitions, a conclusion.
- Anything to quote cards: five to ten lines under thirty words, with
  attribution, one sentence of context, the platform each suits.

## Native, not identical

Each version gets its own hook, its own length, and something the
source did not have. Rewrite for the audience of that channel; never
paste. Stagger over days, not hours. Track which cut sends readers back
to the source (`data/social/snapshots/`, `data/analytics/snapshots/`)
and make more of that kind.

## The advocacy pack

For colleagues who will share: first person, short, an opinion they can
own, no product pitch, the link with the UTMs from
`data/ontology/naming.md`, and a line on what not to claim. Three to
five variants so a feed does not show the same post five times.
