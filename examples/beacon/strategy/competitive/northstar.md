# Northstar

- **What they are:** "Monitoring, alerting and status pages in one place",
  the incumbent suite most of our prospects already pay for.
- **Where we meet them:** every deal where engineering set up the status
  page years ago and support inherited it.

## They win when…

The buyer is engineering, the budget is the monitoring budget, and the
customers are technical enough to read a status page as the whole story.
A developer-tools company with a small support team and patient customers
is a fair Northstar win.

## We win when…

Support owns the budget and the pain: a ticket spike after the last
incident, an escalation from a large account, a renewal that opened with
the outage. Their page stays; our update, audience and report are what
they lacked. Proof: 48 seconds to first update, 31 percent fewer incident
tickets at a design-tool customer, a 64 percent open rate on the report.

## Landmines to plant (fair ones)

- "Who writes the customer update during an incident, and from where?"
- "Can you send a different message to the customers who were affected
  and the ones who were not?"
- "What does the customer receive the morning after?"

## Their common attacks on us, and the honest response

| They say | We say |
| --- | --- |
| "You already have a status page with us." | True. Keep it. The page is where a customer looks; Beacon is what they receive. |
| "Another tool for the team to learn." | Also true. It is the tool the support team learns, instead of the one engineering learned for them. |

## Pricing and packaging (as of 2026-08)

Status pages are bundled with the monitoring plans; no separate public
price. Guess: the bundle makes the page free in the buyer's mind, so we do
not compete on price, we compete on whose job it does.
