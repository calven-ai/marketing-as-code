# The seven sweeps

Condensed from the source above and rewritten for this repo. Run them in
order, one at a time.

1. **Clarity.** Every sentence understood on first read: pronouns with
   a clear referent, no unexplained jargon, no ambiguous claim, no
   missing context. One main idea per section; the copy addresses the
   reader as "you".
2. **Voice and tone.** No drift between formal and casual, no mood
   swings, word choices that match `brand/voice.md`. Read it aloud;
   the jarring line is the one to fix.
3. **So what.** After every statement ask "so what?". A feature with no
   benefit ("uses AI analytics") becomes one with an outcome ("decisions
   in half the time", only if a snapshot says so).
4. **Prove it.** Every claim has proof nearby: a customer quote, a case
   study, a number with a source, a review score, a logo. "Industry
   leading" with no attribution is a finding.
5. **Specificity.** "Save time" to "save four hours a week"; "many
   customers" to the number; "fast results" to "results in 14 days";
   but only when the brief's sources carry the number.
6. **Feeling.** Does the reader feel the cost of the current state and
   the relief of the better one? A concrete "before" moment, a
   micro-story, a shared experience; never a manufactured urgency.
7. **Zero risk.** Objections answered next to the CTA: free trial, no
   card, cancel any time, a guarantee, trust signals, a clear next step.

## Word-level cuts

Very, really, extremely, incredibly, just, actually, basically, "in
order to" (to), "that" when it adds nothing, things, stuff.

| Weak | Plain |
| --- | --- |
| utilize | use |
| implement | set up |
| leverage | use |
| facilitate | help |
| innovative | new |
| robust | strong |
| seamless | smooth |
| cutting-edge | new |

## Sentence and paragraph checks

One idea per sentence; vary length; put the important part first; at
most three conjunctions; usually under 25 words. One topic per
paragraph; two to four sentences on the web; a strong first sentence;
white space between.

## Common problems and the fix

- Wall of features: add "which means" and finish the sentence.
- Corporate speak: ask how a person would say it.
- Weak opening: lead with the reader's problem or the outcome.
- Buried CTA: make the ask obvious, early and repeated.
- No proof: add the number, quote or case.
- Generic claim: who, how, by how much.
- Mixed audiences: pick one and write to them.
- Feature overload: three to five benefits, not ten.

## Scoring a draft

9 to 10 publish-ready; 7 to 8 strong with minor tweaks; 5 to 6 works but
needs another pass; 3 to 4 major revision; 1 to 2 rethink. Give the
number with the reason, not alone.
