# Campaign formats, the agreement outline, the checklist, the metrics

Condensed from the source above.

## Formats

| Family | Format | Effort | Leads |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| content | co-authored post | low | shared byline, link swap |
| content | joint ebook or guide | medium | gated, split |
| content | research report | high | gated, split |
| content | newsletter swap | low | each keeps its own |
| content | podcast guest exchange | low | each keeps its own |

## Finding the idea

Shared audience moments (a trigger event, a season, a trend both customer
bases feel); the combined value proposition (what customers achieve with
both, the workflow the integration enables, the pain the pair removes);
each side's assets (audience, content expertise, product, credibility,
customer stories); the prompts "what would we launch in two weeks", "what
would make customers say finally".

## Agreement outline, eight parts

1. The campaign, in a paragraph.
2. Responsibilities: who produces what.
3. Timeline: phases and deadlines.
4. Lead handling: capture, sharing, follow-up.
5. Promotion commitments: the minimum from each side.
6. Branding: logo use and approvals.
7. Budget: the split, if any.
8. Metrics sharing: what each reports after, and when.

## Checklist by phase

Identify: map customer stacks; check account overlap; score the top five;
read their co-marketing history. Plan: pick the format and the goal;
agree lead sharing; assign tasks with dates; define success up front.
Execute: co-create the shared assets (page, content); synchronise the
promotion calendar; brief both teams on the messaging. After: share the
numbers with the partner; a lessons-learned conversation; decide on the
next one.

## Metrics

Quantitative: leads in total and per partner; lead quality by the funnel
definition; revenue attributed; audience growth; content engagement.
Qualitative: ease of collaboration; responsiveness; how the audience
received it; brand lift; the relationship's strength for a next campaign.
