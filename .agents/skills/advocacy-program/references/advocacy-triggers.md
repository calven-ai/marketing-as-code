# Advocacy triggers

Condensed from the source above: the three moments a B2B customer-success
motion turns into an advocacy ask, and why sales cares.

## The three triggers

1. **NPS promoter (9 or 10)**: open a reference request. Within two weeks
   of the survey, from the account owner, with the specific ask (a call
   with a prospect in their industry, a quote, a review).
2. **Expansion closed**: ask for a case study within 30 days, while the
   business case that justified the expansion is fresh and the numbers
   are the customer's own.
3. **A referral received**: attribute it to the account and its owner,
   and raise the account's advocate standing so the next ask goes there
   first.

## The flywheel

Expansion revenue, satisfied customers, advocacy, referral pipeline, new
customers, expansion again. A reference roster and published case studies
feed proposals and shorten cycles; that is why sales, not only marketing,
should own part of the ask list.

## External benchmarks the source cites

Advocacy programs at several times the revenue of paid advertising;
referred customers around a quarter higher lifetime value; referred
prospects converting far above other channels; references shortening deal
cycles by up to a third. Vendor-published figures. Use them to argue for
the program, never as this team's targets; the team's numbers come from
`data/crm/snapshots/`.
