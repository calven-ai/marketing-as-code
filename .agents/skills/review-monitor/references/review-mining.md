# Mining reviews

Condensed from the source above; rewritten for the monthly review report.

## Read order

1. **3-star** first. The customer stayed and named the gap; the most honest
   text on the site.
2. **1-star**: failure modes and breaking points. Read for what broke, not
   for the anger.
3. **5-star**: the language people use for what they value. Copy the
   phrasing, not the sentiment.
4. **4-star**: praise with an "I wish it could..." buried in it. That
   clause is the finding.

## What to take from each review

| Field | Question it answers |
| --- | --- |
| Job to be done | why they adopted the product at all |
| Top praise | the feature or outcome they name first |
| Top complaint | the frustration or missing capability |
| Switching context | what they used before, and why they left it |
| Unmet need | "I wish", "it would be better if", "still no" |

Tag each finding with one of `pain`, `trigger`, `outcome`, `language`,
`alternative`, `objection`, `competitor`. Keep the running table
(source, date, quote, tags, note) inside the report's Evidence section;
patterns are real after about 20 to 30 tagged rows, so a month with fewer
reviews reports "emerging" themes, not findings.

## Competitor reviews

G2 and Capterra split each review into "what do you like best", "what do
you dislike" and "what problems are you solving". Read competitors'
4-star reviews first: strengths in the first field, weaknesses in the
second, the job in the third. That is the raw material for
`strategy/competitive/` battlecards; propose the update, do not edit the
battlecard from here.

## Bias

Reviewers are people with strong opinions, in both directions; the silent
middle does not post. Say so in the report's Caveats. Weight the last
twelve months over older reviews, and never let a rating move on fewer
than a handful of reviews read as a trend.
