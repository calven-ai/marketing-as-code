# Win/loss program: evidence lanes, taxonomy, routing

Condensed from the win/loss program in the B2B RevOps skills. The
taxonomy is the one the win/loss skill codes against; the lanes say what
the agent can do alone (lane 1) and what needs a person (lanes 2 and 3).

## Why a program, not a dropdown

Roughly four of five worked opportunities end in a decision the company
never investigates. CRM loss fields cluster on "price" and "timing";
buyer interviews surface trust, capability gaps, champion strength and
decisions that were rigged before the evaluation started. The program
replaces the field with investigation of decided deals, never live ones.

## Three evidence lanes

1. Transcript and thread mining, every deal (the agent's lane). Within a
   week of the decision, read calls, emails and proposals; extract the
   buyer's verbatim words on problems, alternatives, budget, timing and
   politics; rebuild the real decision timeline (it rarely matches the CRM
   date); compare the rep's stated reason to the documents. Limit: it only
   holds what was said to you, not what happened in their internal
   meetings.
2. Buyer interviews, sampled (a person's lane). Large and strategic deals
   plus a rotating sample, wins included because wins reveal the real
   differentiators. Two to six weeks after the decision, a neutral
   interviewer who was not on the deal, thirty minutes. Spine question:
   "walk me through the decision as it actually happened". Closing
   question: "what would have had to be true for this to go the other
   way?" Write down the uncomfortable answers without defending.
3. Customer arena, group sessions (a person's lane). Five to eight
   customers, deliberately mixed (happy, near churn, sceptical); the
   company listens and does not defend or promise roadmap; walk the
   journey chronologically; tag findings product or process. One or two a
   year per segment.

## Decision-driver taxonomy

Code every deal against these, a primary and a secondary driver, so the
quarters compare:

| Driver | Covers |
| --- | --- |
| Product capability gap | A thing they needed that we do not do, or do worse |
| Trust and proof | References, security, viability, credibility of claims |
| Price to value | Not "too expensive" but value not believed at that price |
| Champion strength | Nobody inside carried it, or the champion lost |
| Competitive move | A competitor's specific action, offer or relationship |
| Timing and budget reality | Budget vanished, priorities moved, no critical event |
| Process failure | Our sales process: slow, wrong people, bad demo, missed steps |

Single-cause coding recreates the dropdown problem; two drivers per deal
is the minimum.

## Synthesis and routing

A quarterly cross-functional readout anchored to quotes; one page each for
sales (process), product (capabilities), marketing (language), leadership
(trends). Route outputs: loss patterns to a revival list; objections to
enablement; competitive findings to battlecards; systemic causes to
qualification gates. In this repo: the report, the themes knowledge file,
`battlecard`, `messaging-house`, `sales-enablement-kit`, and the decision
log.

## Success indicators

The CRM loss field stops driving decisions; positioning and roadmap choices
cite interview evidence; at least one finding per quarter contradicts what
the company believes about itself; a side-by-side of CRM reasons and
interview findings shows real divergence.

## Benchmark

Average B2B win rate roughly 19 to 21 percent (Ebsta and Pavilion GTM
benchmarks, 2025). Use it as context, not as a target; the team's own
quarter-over-quarter line is the number that matters.
