# Expansion signals and scoring

Condensed from the source above; the parts a quarterly expansion list
uses. Benchmarks are the source's, calibrated for B2B SaaS between about
15 and 150 million ARR; adjust for stage and deal size, and never present
them as this team's numbers.

## Retention first

Expansion built on churn hides the churn. Read gross revenue retention
before ranking anyone:

| GRR | Read | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| below 85 percent | critical | stop expansion work; the list is a retention list |
| 85 to 90 | concerning | expansion masks structural churn; name the top three churn drivers first |
| 90 to 95 | healthy | expansion architecture is worth building |
| above 95 | excellent | maximise expansion velocity |

The source puts the private-SaaS median at 88 to 91 percent and the top
quartile above 95. NRR above 110 is good, above 120 best in class.

## Signals

- **Usage**: above 80 percent of seat or usage limits; a new feature
  adopted; active users up more than 20 percent month on month; a new
  user persona appearing.
- **Relationship**: a champion promoted or a new sponsor engaged; NPS
  trending up; a reference or case study accepted; voluntary event
  attendance; new stakeholders asking for access.
- **Commercial**: headcount growth at the account; a budget cycle
  approaching; a multi-year renewal coming up; interest from another
  function.
- **Outcome**: value delivered ahead of plan; ROI above the business case;
  the customer asking about new use cases.

## Readiness score, 0 to 100

| Component | Weight | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Usage growth | 30 | monthly growth, proximity to limits, new features adopted |
| Stakeholder breadth | 25 | new users, departments involved, executive participation |
| Feature depth | 20 | advanced features in use, higher-tier requests |
| Health trajectory | 15 | three-month trend, NPS movement, tickets falling |
| Contract headroom | 10 | room to expand, renewal timing, unused rights |

When a snapshot for a component is missing, drop the component and
rescale the rest to 100; say which were dropped.

| Score | Action |
| --- | --- |
| 0 to 30 | monitor; focus on adoption |
| 31 to 50 | seed: the owner shares relevant content |
| 51 to 70 | qualify: the owner opens the conversation |
| 71 to 100 | pursue; above 30 percent of current ACV, hand to sales |

## Ownership by deal size

Seat growth under 10 percent and small upsells under 20 percent of ACV
stay with customer success; 20 to 50 percent is sourced by CS and closed
by sales; above 50 percent, a new product line or a new buying group is a
sales deal with CS support. CS packages the opportunity (champion, why
now, the need, the original business case) and stays through close;
sourced credit to CS, closed credit to sales.

## Decision rules worth copying into the report

- Score above 70: the owner opens the conversation within five days.
- Usage above 80 percent of plan for two months: an upgrade conversation.
- Champion promoted to VP or above: an executive meeting within two weeks.
- Health green for six months and whitespace above 50 percent: a growth
  plan in the next QBR.
- NRR under 110 for a segment: a strategy question, churn or expansion
  gap, not a bigger list.

## Pricing as an expansion lever

The source's bands: flat-rate pricing tends to 100 to 110 percent NRR,
tiered 105 to 115, usage-based 110 to 130, hybrid 115 to 125. An
expansion list cannot fix a pricing model that never expands on its own;
note it in Caveats when the plan structure is flat.
