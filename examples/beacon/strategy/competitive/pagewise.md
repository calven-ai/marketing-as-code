# Pagewise

- **What they are:** "A beautiful status page in five minutes", a
  standalone, self-serve status page tool with public pricing.
- **Where we meet them:** smaller developer-tools teams, and in AI answers:
  Pagewise is named in 6 of our 12 buyer prompts (2026-09-30 check), Beacon
  in 3.

## They win when…

The team wants a status page and nothing else. A developer-tools company
under 50 people, with technical customers, no support team to speak of
and a founder who writes the outage email, should buy Pagewise. It is
cheap, quick to set up and well made.

## We win when…

There is a support team, and it is the one paying for the last incident:
the ticket spike from customers who were never affected, the account
manager rewriting the update for a large customer, the report nobody
wrote. Pagewise has no audience segments and no post-incident report.
Proof: 31 percent fewer incident tickets at a design-tool customer after
one quarter, 48 seconds to first update, 64 percent open rate on the
report.

## Landmines to plant (fair ones)

- "Can you send an update only to the customers on one plan or in one
  region?"
- "Who writes the email and the in-app message, and do they say the same
  thing as the page?"
- "What does a customer's account manager send the next morning?"

## Their common attacks on us, and the honest response

| They say | We say |
| --- | --- |
| "Beacon costs many times more." | For one page, yes. Beacon is priced for the support team's whole incident, and the tickets from the wrong audience cost more than the difference. |
| "Set up in five minutes, not a week." | Fair: segments need account data, so setup takes a day. The trial syncs it from the support tool. |

## Pricing and packaging (as of 2026-09)

Public: from 29 USD per month for one page, self-serve with a card, and higher
tiers for more subscribers and custom domains. No sales team that we have
seen. We do not compete on price; we qualify out teams that only want the
page.
