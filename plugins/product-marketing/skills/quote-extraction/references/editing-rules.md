# Editing and anchoring rules

Open this file before you write the first quote, and again before you re-anchor one.

## A quote is a passage you found

Start from ONE unbroken stretch of the source, inside one speaker's turn, and tidy it in place. Every word you keep is a word that is there, in the order it is there.

**You may:**

- fix spelling, grammar, tense and agreement;
- fix capitalisation and punctuation;
- remove filler and disfluencies: `um`, `uh`, `you know`, `like`, `I mean`, `sort of`, `kind of`, `right?`, false starts, words said twice;
- spell out the company's name and rivals' names in full, and correct a misheard name.

**You may never:**

- add a word, a clause or a parenthetical that is not in the passage;
- reorder what was said;
- summarise the ending in your own words;
- join two stretches that are not next to each other;
- swap a word for one a native speaker would prefer ("the tool is very much complicated" stays as it is, or pick another stretch).

Example: `Yeah so um the onboarding it was kind of painful honestly` becomes `The onboarding was painful.`

## The ending is where it goes wrong

A trailing clause you rephrase instead of copying makes the quote impossible to find in the call.

- Passage: *"we post on the status page, and then, like, the email, that's someone writing it by hand, every time, and it goes to everybody"*. Wrong: `We post on the status page, but the email is still hand-written and sent to all customers.` (nothing after "page" is in that form). Right: `We post on the status page, and then the email, that's someone writing it by hand, every time, and it goes to everybody.`
- Passage: *"it cost us, I'll throw out a number, about forty grand in credits last quarter, the SLA credits"*. Wrong: `It cost us about $40k in credits last quarter (SLA credits).` (the parenthetical is yours). Right: `It cost us about $40k in credits last quarter, the SLA credits.`

If tidying would mean guessing what they meant, stay closer to the passage or leave the quote out.

## Machine transcripts of live speech

Run-on sentences, false starts and misheard words are normal. The best quote is usually the shortest unbroken stretch, often one or two sentences, that carries the point once filler is gone. Two good sentences with other talk between them are two quotes, or one quote with the first in the context note, never one quote. If no stretch reads cleanly after deleting filler and fixing grammar, skip it: a quote that reads like raw transcription is worth less than no quote.

## One idea, self-contained

- One claim per quote. Split a multi-part statement; never staple unrelated points together.
- Prefer a stretch that names its own subject: start where the speaker names the thing, not at the "it" that follows. When the subject was named earlier, never add it to the quote; say what it refers to in the context note.
- A bare "Yes" or "Exactly" is never a quote. A short answer is one only when the question it answers goes in the context note.
- Keep each quote under about 600 characters (100 words). When the stretch runs longer, quote its strongest unbroken part. Drop anything over 1,000 characters.

## Context note

Say why the quote was said, not what it says: asked directly, volunteered, conceded under pushback, and what any "it", "that" or "they" refers to by name ("on Northstar, their current status page"). One short clause. Leave it empty only when the quote is fully self-explanatory. Every publishable quote gets one.

## Re-anchoring a quote that drifted

When a quote's words cannot be found in order in the source, find the stretch it came from and rebuild it in two steps.

1. **Copy the source stretch** character for character, filler and punctuation included, inside one speaker's turn, never across a speaker label. If the statement spans two turns, take the turn that carries the substance.
2. **Edit it in two rounds.**
   - *Delete:* filler, false starts, repeated words, asides with no claim (`to be honest`, `as I said`). Never delete a negation (`not`, `never`, `don't`), a number, a name, a hedge (`maybe`, `probably`, `about`, `I think`), a modal (`will`, `would`, `can`, `could`, `might`, `should`) or a condition (`if`, `unless`). You may end the stretch early at the end of a sentence.
   - *Fix grammar locally:* expand `gonna` and `wanna`; fix a word ending (plural, agreement, tense); add or change a small grammar word (an article, `is/are/was`, `do/have`, a preposition, `there/it/that`); fix a misheard name; write numbers as numbers (`10 to 20 k` becomes `$10k to $20k`); fix punctuation.

   Every other word stays as spoken, in order. Do not swap words, change a pronoun, change a modal or a condition (`will` to `would`, `if` to `when`, `and` to `or`), or join sentences the speaker kept apart. If the result reads a little awkwardly, that is correct: faithful beats polished.

Worked re-anchor:

- Drifted quote: `Our agents spend the first hour of every incident answering whether we're down.`
- Source stretch: `so the agents, like, the first hour, they're they're just answering "are you down", "are you down", that's the whole first hour basically`
- Re-anchored: `The agents, the first hour, they're just answering "are you down". That's the whole first hour.`

If the source does not contain the statement, drop the quote.

## Pairing with the original language

Take one unbroken stretch of the original, tidy it the same way, cut it at the same boundaries as the English quote, stay inside one turn. Never translate the English back. No matching stretch means no original.
