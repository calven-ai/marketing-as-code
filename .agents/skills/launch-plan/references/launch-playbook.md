# Launch playbook: readiness, phases, channels, checklist

Condensed from the launch skill in marketingskills. The tier matrix is the
part this repo's launch plan uses first; the phases and checklist feed the
timeline and the task list.

## Readiness gate

Ship when the thing is simple (solves one clearly defined problem),
lovable (people want it, not tolerate it) and complete (a whole experience
inside its scope). The two failure modes are launching too late out of
perfectionism and never launching because scope keeps growing.

## Tiers: how much launch a change deserves

| Tier | What | Treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Major | New product, new market, an overhaul | Full multi-channel campaign, all phases below |
| Medium | A notable feature, an integration, a UI change | Targeted email, in-app note, blog, social, sales note |
| Minor | Fixes and small improvements | Changelog entry only |

Stagger releases so there is always something to talk about, and reuse
what worked last time.

## Five phases

1. Internal: one-to-one with friendly users; validate the core.
2. Alpha: controlled external access from a landing page; start a waitlist.
3. Beta: scaled early access; teasers; recruit people who will share.
4. Early access: leak screenshots, GIFs, demos; gather data; widen
   gradually or all at once.
5. Full launch: open self-serve, charge if applicable, announce everywhere.

## Channels: owned, rented, borrowed

- Owned (email list, blog, community, website): compound over time and
  survive algorithm changes; start with one or two that match how the
  audience wants to hear from you.
- Rented (social, app stores, video, forums): fast reach, platform risk;
  use them to drive people back to owned channels, never as the base.
- Borrowed (guest content, partners, speaking, influencers): find audience
  overlap, pitch a win-win, set up an incentive.

## Product Hunt, when it fits

Before: relationships with people who will share, a tight listing
(tagline, visuals, demo video), study of past launches, community warm-up,
a team ready for the whole day. On the day: answer comments in real time,
send traffic to owned channels. After: turn the traffic into email
relationships.

## Post-launch

Onboarding email sequence; the announcement repeated in the regular
roundup; comparison pages for the competitors it displaces; an interactive
demo; feature sections added across the website.

## Checklist

Pre-launch: landing page, email capture, early access list, channels
set up, assets made, analytics tracking, onboarding flow.
Launch day: announcement email, blog post, social posts, listing if any,
in-app announcement, website banner, team on standby.
Post-launch: onboarding sequence live, prospect follow-up, comparison
pages, demo, feedback collection.

## Examples the source cites

Invite-only scarcity (Superhuman), community-driven spread (Notion), one
organic partnership that carried a hardware launch (TRMNL), and Product
Hunt done with preparation (SavvyCal, Reform). Patterns, not recipes.
