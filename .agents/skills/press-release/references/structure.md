# Press release structure

Condensed from the source above.

## Sections and lengths

| Section | Job | Length |
| --- | --- | --- |
| headline | the news itself | 10 to 15 words |
| subhead | the key detail or context | 15 to 20 words |
| dateline | city, date | one line |
| lead paragraph | who, what, when, where, why | 2 to 3 sentences |
| body | detail, context, significance | 2 to 4 paragraphs |
| quote 1 | the executive's perspective | 2 to 3 sentences |
| quote 2 | a customer or partner's validation | 2 to 3 sentences |
| boilerplate | who the company is | 3 to 4 sentences |
| media contact | name, email, phone | one block |

Lead formula: "`<Company>` today announced `<what>`, enabling `<who>` to
`<benefit>`."

## The news test

- Something happened; existence is not news.
- A reader outside the company cares.
- Specific numbers, dates or names are in it.
- It is current or imminent.
- A credible third-party voice speaks in it.
- The headline alone conveys the news.
- One sentence explains why it matters.

## Headlines

Strong: a specific number or amount, a clear difference, proof, a
data angle. Weak: "exciting", buzzwords, self-congratulation, no news.

## Quotes

A quote adds a perspective the body does not carry, sounds like a person,
expresses a view or an intent, and is specific to this announcement. Cut
quotes that restate the body, use corporate platitudes, or could sit in
any release.

## Boilerplate pattern

"About `<Company>`: `<one-sentence description>`. Founded `<year>`,
`<the difference>`. Trusted by `<proof>`. Headquartered in `<city>`.
`<website>`." Keep one boilerplate for every release; change it through
the `positioning-refresh` cascade, not in a release.

## Timing

Tuesday to Wednesday, 6 to 8 in the morning in the outlet's time zone.
Not Monday (inbox overload), not Friday (buried by the weekend).

## Types

Product launch, funding, partnership, executive hire, milestone, research
or data, acquisition, award.

## Anti-patterns

News buried past paragraph one; the news only inside a quote; jargon;
length for its own sake; no statement of significance; an unproven
"first in the industry"; several quotes saying the same thing; no
follow-up after the embargo lifts.
