# Page checklists

Condensed from the source above and rewritten for this repo. Use them
after the strategy and voice checks, never instead of them.

## Quick quality check, any page

- Jargon the persona would not use?
- Sentences carrying more than one idea?
- Passive constructions ("reports are generated")?
- Exclamation marks?
- Empty words: streamline, optimize, innovative, seamless, robust?

## Style rules

| Prefer | Over |
| --- | --- |
| simple ("use") | complex ("utilize") |
| specific (a number, a timeframe) | vague ("faster") |
| active | passive |
| confident | qualified ("almost", "very", "really") |
| shown outcomes | adverbs |
| honest | sensational; no invented stats or testimonials |

## Above the fold

Headline with one core value proposition that passes "now you can";
subheadline of one or two sentences that makes it specific; one
primary CTA that says what the visitor gets.

## Section order that usually works

Social proof, problem in the reader's words, three to five benefits
tied to outcomes, how it works in three or four steps, objections
(FAQ, comparison, guarantee), final CTA with the value restated and the
risk removed. A page that skips proof and objections is a finding.

## CTAs

Weak: Submit, Sign up, Learn more, Click here, Get started. Strong:
verb plus what they get plus a qualifier ("Start my free trial", "See
pricing for my team"). One primary CTA per page; the same verb every
time it repeats.

## Per page type

- Homepage: several audiences without going generic; the broadest
  value proposition first; clear paths for each visitor intent.
- Landing page: one message, one CTA, headline matched to the ad or
  email that sent the visitor, the whole argument on one page.
- Pricing: helps a visitor pick a plan, answers "which one is for me",
  marks the recommended option, states what changes between tiers.
- Feature page: feature to benefit to customer outcome, a use case, a
  concrete example, a clear path to trial or purchase.
- About: the founding "why", tied to the customer's benefit, with a
  CTA.

## Before writing a rewrite

Know the page's single purpose, the persona and their objection, what
is being offered and how it differs, the traffic source and what the
visitor already knows on arrival. In this repo those come from
`strategy/` and from the piece's brief, never from guesswork.

## Priorities when two rules collide

Clarity over cleverness; benefits over features; specific over vague;
the customer's words over ours; one idea per section.
