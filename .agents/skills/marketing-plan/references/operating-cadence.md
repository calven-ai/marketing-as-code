# The cadence that keeps a plan honest

Condensed from the source above; the parts a marketing plan uses to say
how its OKRs will be read, by whom, and how often.

## The five-P gate for any recurring meeting

Purpose (one outcome, not "discuss"); Product (an artifact: decisions
logged, a list, a dashboard updated); People (roles, not spectators);
Process (a two-to-five-line agenda with timeboxes and a decision-maker);
Proof (the data delivered 24 hours before, so the meeting decides
instead of discovering). A meeting failing a P is cancelled or becomes an
async update; a healthy calendar cancels 5 to 15 percent of them.

## A cadence that fits this repo

| Rhythm | Reads | Produces |
| --- | --- | --- |
| weekly | `weekly-report`: what shipped, headline numbers, risks | up to three decisions, each with an owner and a date |
| weekly | the roles' recurring reports (pipeline, web, search, paid, customer) | adjusted programs, experiments with a kill rule |
| monthly | the `-monthly` weekly edition against the OKR table | updated priorities, the reforecast question |
| quarterly | `qmr` against the plan of record | a refreshed OKR table, targets confirmed or changed, decisions logged |

Coaching happens inside these reviews, not as a separate meeting.

## Signal rules instead of more meetings

Pick eight to twelve signals, set thresholds from six months of history,
and write down which review each one escalates to (for example: pipeline
coverage under 2.5 times for three weeks goes to the weekly demand
review). Publish the map next to the dashboard.

## Health of the cadence itself, monthly

Decision latency under seven days from insight to a live countermeasure;
a 5 to 15 percent cancel rate; over 80 percent of actions done on time;
a kill rate of 20 percent or more of experiments per quarter; data
freshness checks passing.

## Anti-patterns

No pre-read (the first fifteen minutes are the pre-read); reviews with
no logged action; forecasts treated as a negotiation; forty metrics on a
dashboard (three to five per review, each tied to a decision); no
follow-up on last time's actions; dirty CRM data underneath it all.

## The ten-minute board answer

Are we on track; why (win rate by segment, top three loss reasons); what
changed since last time; what we need, with the return; the top three
risks. The monthly edition of the weekly report is built to answer these.
