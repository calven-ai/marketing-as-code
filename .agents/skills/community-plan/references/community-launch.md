# Community launch: journey, calendar, governance, metrics

## Foundations

Three pillars: a shared purpose (why people join), real connection (why
they stay), member empowerment (how it sustains itself). A community is
many-to-many; an audience is one-to-many. The flywheel: attract,
activate, engage, advocate.

| Stage | Size | Priority |
| --- | --- | --- |
| nascent | under 100 | one-to-one conversations, manual operations |
| growing | 100 to 1,000 | systems, rituals, first programs |
| scaling | 1,000 to 10,000 | governance, delegation, moderation |
| mature | over 10,000 | sub-communities, ecosystem |

## Home base and outposts

One home base, committed to for six to twelve months (Slack, Discord,
Circle, Discourse); outposts for discovery where members already gather
(LinkedIn, X, Reddit, GitHub Discussions). A channel architecture that
has worked: info (announcements, FAQ, rules), core (general,
introductions), support (help, suggestions), gated (a members-only or
customers-only space), live (AMAs, office hours).

## The member journey

Aware, join, lurk, first post, regular contributor, champion, ambassador.
The 1-9-90 rule: 1% create, 9% contribute, 90% read; the goal is to move
people one step, not to make everyone a creator. First wins per stage: a
lurker's first reaction; a newcomer connecting with a peer by day three;
a regular's first contribution by week two; a champion leading something
by month two.

## Eight steps to launch

1. Intake: the persona, the job the community does (support, activation,
   retention, advocacy, feedback), where members gather today,
   constraints.
2. Thesis and value exchange: what the community stands for; what
   members get and what they give.
3. Model and journey: support, learning, networking, advocacy or
   co-creation; measurable signals per stage.
4. Platform and outposts; a shortlist of people whose networks seed it.
5. Seed in 48 hours: a founding cohort of 25 to 100, a welcome message
   and a start-here guide, an introductions prompt with human replies,
   pre-seeded discussions so nobody arrives in an empty room.
6. Programming for four to eight weeks: a weekly rhythm with themed
   days, staff-led and member-led moments, AMAs, office hours,
   challenges, show-and-tell.
7. Ambassadors (optional, recommended): qualification, responsibilities
   (moderation, content, referrals), recognition (badges, shout-outs,
   early access).
8. Governance: five to seven rules; an enforcement ladder (warn, mute,
   ban); daily 15-minute moderation check, reports answered within 24
   hours; a metrics dashboard.

## Metrics and red flags

| Category | Metric | Target |
| --- | --- | --- |
| growth | new members, churn, referral rate | trend |
| engagement | daily to monthly active, posts per member | over 20% |
| health | monthly retention, sentiment | over 80% retained |
| value | NPS, support deflection, product feedback shipped | NPS over 50 |

Red flags: active ratio under 10% (re-engage); the top ten members over
80% of posts (a "new voices" push); sentiment falling (review moderation
and the response plan).

## Anti-patterns

Build it and wait; vanity metrics; over-engineering early; ignoring
lurkers; an absent founder in the first months; automated DMs or
manipulation; scaling before engagement exists.

## The plan's deliverables

Context and assumptions; thesis and value exchange; journey map with
metrics; platform and outposts; a 30/60/90 seeding and onboarding plan; a
four-to-eight-week programming calendar; ambassador program v1;
governance and moderation; the dashboard and the weekly rhythm; risks,
open questions, next steps.
