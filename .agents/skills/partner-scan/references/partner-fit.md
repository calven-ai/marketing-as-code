# Partner fit: who to look for, how to score, what to run with them

Condensed from the source above.

## The ideal partner

Same buyer persona, a different problem solved; adjacent in the workflow
(before, after or alongside the product); a similar stage and customer
size; complementary, never competitive.

Discovery questions: what tools do our customers already use; what do they
use before and after us; who else sells to our ICP; which integrations do
customers ask for most.

## Scoring matrix, 1 to 5 each

| Criterion | What you are judging |
| --- | --- |
| audience fit | overlap with our ICP |
| audience size | enough reach to be worth the work |
| brand alignment | would we be proud of the association |
| engagement quality | an active, responsive audience, not a large dormant one |
| reciprocity | an equal exchange is possible |
| ease of execution | a partnerships person exists; co-marketing history |

Sum to 30. Evidence per score: a customer page, a newsletter or event
audience they publish, past joint campaigns, a partner page.

## Partnership types

| Type | Definition | Payoff |
| --- | --- | --- |
| integration | a product connection | retention, expansion, discovery |
| co-marketing | joint content and campaigns with peers | borrowed audience, brand halo |
| referral or affiliate | commission-based promotion | pay for performance |
| reseller | they sell the product with services | distribution and revenue |
| marketplace | an ecosystem listing | built-in distribution |

Mature partner programmes average roughly 28 percent of revenue; HubSpot's
program was cited at about 40 percent.

## Formats by effort

| Format | Effort | Leads | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| co-authored post | low | link swap | thought leadership, SEO |
| newsletter swap | low | each keeps its own | exposure |
| podcast guest exchange | low | each keeps its own | relationship |
| "better together" page | low | shared | integration discovery |
| joint webinar | medium | shared | leads, education |
| joint ebook or guide | medium | gated, split | depth, leads |
| integration launch | medium | shared | existing integration partners |
| joint case study | medium | shared | shared customers |
| bundle or discount | medium | | conversion, cross-sell |
| booth share | medium | | cost split at an event |
| research report | high | gated, split | authority, PR |
| co-hosted workshop | high | | hands-on engagement |

## Finding the campaign

Shared audience moments (trigger events, seasons, trends that hit both
customer bases); the combined value (what both tools enable that neither
does alone); an asset inventory on each side (audience, content, product,
credibility, customer stories); the prompts "what would we launch in two
weeks", "what do both audiences need", "what data story could we tell
together".

## The first note to a partner (a person sends it)

Subject "`<us>` + `<them>` co-marketing idea"; your role and one line on
the company; the specific audience overlap you observed; one sentence of
campaign; an invitation to an exploratory call. Bring overlap data, two
or three ideas, your audience numbers, past examples, and a clear ask.
