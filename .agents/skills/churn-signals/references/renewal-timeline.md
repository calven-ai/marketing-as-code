# The renewal timeline and the save levers

Condensed from the source above. The weekly list classifies each account
by where it sits on this timeline and which failure mode it shows.

## Four checkpoints

| Days before renewal | What must be true |
| --- | --- |
| 120 | an owner is named; a health verdict exists (clean, watch, at risk) from usage trend, support pattern and champion status |
| 90 | at-risk accounts are in a save motion; watch accounts got a value recap and are re-verdicted at 60; clean accounts have a renewal packet drafted |
| 60 | commercial terms are final; if the price is changing, the customer heard it by now |
| 30 | signature logistics only; an objection this late is an upstream failure, not a save problem |

A renewal inside 120 days with no owner is itself a signal.

## Five failure modes, each with its first play

| Mode | What it looks like | First play |
| --- | --- | --- |
| value gap | used, but nobody can justify the cost | an impact story in the customer's own numbers, to the economic buyer |
| adoption gap | low or no usage, launch never finished | shrink scope to the one painful workflow and relaunch narrowly |
| champion loss | the contact who bought it left | treat as a new sale to whoever inherited the problem; a separate track for the departed champion |
| sponsor shift | reorg or a new decision-maker | executive to executive, re-anchored on the new agenda |
| competitive | an active evaluation elsewhere | the switching-cost argument plus a senior-led roadmap commitment with dates |

## Commercial levers, in order

1. Scope right-sizing: fewer seats or modules at a defensible value.
2. Term restructure: shorter with success gates, or longer with protections.
3. Service injection: implementation, training, a success plan (a one-off
   cost, unlike a discount that reprices every future renewal).
4. Pause instead of cancel, where the product allows it.
5. Price concession, last, and always traded for something: a case
   study, term, an expansion option, a reference.

Discounts buy silence, not adoption.

## Release or fight

Some accounts should churn. Score fit, problem and economics; two of three
or better, fight; otherwise release cleanly with a data handover and a
standing way back.

## What to measure over time

Save rate by failure mode, not blended; days from first signal to first
human action; concession cost per saved dollar. The source cites private
SaaS GRR median around 90 percent and top quartile above 95, and a
retention-versus-acquisition cost gap of five times and up: external
numbers, for context only.

## Diagnostic questions for the team

Does every renewal inside 120 days have an owner? For the last five
churns, how many days passed between the first signal in the data and the
first human action? What happened at the next renewal to accounts saved
with a discount? What fires automatically when a champion leaves? Can the
team produce a value recap for its top ten accounts within a day?
