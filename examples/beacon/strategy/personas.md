# Personas

Two personas: the economic buyer and the champion who brings Beacon to her.

## Priya Nair (Buyer, economic buyer)

Owns customer support at a 200-person design-tool company, reports to the
COO, and signs for the support team's tools. During an incident she is the
person the CEO messages.

- **Role / title:** Head of Customer Support
- **Seniority:** Director
- **Department:** Support
- **Buying role:** economic buyer

**Company objectives / initiatives**

| Objective / initiative | How this persona advances it |
| --- | --- |
| Net revenue retention above 110 percent | Keeps enterprise accounts from churning after a bad quarter of incidents |

**Goals & KPIs**

| KPI | Definition | Target |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Incident ticket volume | Tickets opened during and in the two hours after an incident | Down by a third this year |
| CSAT after incidents | Satisfaction score on tickets tagged "outage" | Above 80 percent |

**Pains**

| Pain | Description | Impact |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The blank page | An incident opens and nobody knows what to write to customers | High: the first update is late and sounds defensive |
| Every customer gets the alarm | One status page, one email list, so unaffected customers panic too | High: half the incident tickets come from people who were fine |

**Gains (desired outcomes)**

| Gain | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| A first update in a minute, in the company's voice | The CEO stops messaging her during incidents |
| A report she can send the next morning | Renewals stop opening with the outage |

**Jobs to be done**

| Job | Type |
| --- | --- |
| Keep customers informed during an incident without pulling agents off the queue | Functional |
| Look in control in front of the executive team | Social |

**Objections**

| Objection | Response |
| --- | --- |
| "Engineering already set up a status page." | Keep it. Beacon is for the part engineering does not do: the audience, the voice, the report. |

**Messaging hooks** (canonical copy lives in the messaging file)

- Your customers noticed before your monitoring did. Make sure they hear
  from you next.

**Watering holes:** the Support Driven community, the Support Leaders
newsletter, customer-support tracks at SaaS conferences, LinkedIn.

**Words they use:** "we went dark", "the ticket spike", "the postmortem
nobody reads", "I found out from a customer".

## Marcus Hale (User, champion)

Leads a shift of six to ten agents at a SaaS company of 100 to 300 people
and reports to the Head of Support. When an incident opens he runs the
queue and writes the customer updates by hand, between tickets. He finds
tools himself, tries them in a trial, and takes the one that worked to his
manager.

- **Role / title:** Support Team Lead
- **Seniority:** Manager
- **Department:** Support
- **Buying role:** champion

**Company objectives / initiatives**

| Objective / initiative | How this persona advances it |
| --- | --- |
| Keep first response time inside target during incidents | Fewer duplicate tickets keep his agents on the queue that matters |

**Goals & KPIs**

| KPI | Definition | Target |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First response time during incidents | Median time to first agent reply on tickets opened during an incident | Under 30 minutes |
| Time to first customer update | Minutes from incident opened to the first public update | Under 5 minutes |

**Pains**

| Pain | Description | Impact |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Writing updates by hand | He drafts the status page post, the email and the macro reply himself, three times, mid-incident | High: the queue grows while he types |
| "Is it down?" tickets | Customers who were never affected write in because they got the alarm | High: his agents answer the same question a hundred times |

**Gains (desired outcomes)**

| Gain | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| One update that goes everywhere | He gets back to the queue in a minute |
| A template he trusts | No one has to wonder whether the wording is approved |

**Jobs to be done**

| Job | Type |
| --- | --- |
| Get the first update out without leaving the queue | Functional |
| Not be the person who sent the wrong message to the wrong customers | Emotional |
| Bring his manager a tool that already works, with numbers | Social |

**Objections**

| Objection | Response |
| --- | --- |
| "I don't have time to set up another tool." | Templates are written once; a trial workspace syncs accounts from the support tool on the first day. |
| "My manager will say we already have a status page." | Keep it. Bring her the ticket count from the last incident and the 31 percent from a team like yours. |

**Messaging hooks** (canonical copy lives in the messaging file)

- Write the update once. It reaches the page, the product and the inbox.
- The customers who were affected hear from you. The others never knew.

**Watering holes:** the Support Driven community and its Slack, r/talesfromtechsupport
and r/CustomerSuccess, support-tool user groups, YouTube walkthroughs.

**Words they use:** "the queue", "macro", "is it down?", "copy-paste the
update", "we got slammed".
