# Email families for the map

Condensed from the email-types reference above. Every automated email
belongs to one family; a stage with none of the families it usually
carries is a gap to name.

| Family | Emails in it | Usual stage | What to measure |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Onboarding | new-user series, new-customer series, step reminders, invite prompts | signup to activation | activation rate, feature adoption |
| Retention | upgrade prompts, review requests, proactive support, usage reports, NPS, referral asks | customer | clicks, replies, NPS response |
| Billing | annual conversion, payment recovery, cancellation survey, renewal reminders | customer, at risk | recovery rate, renewal rate |
| Usage | daily, weekly or monthly summaries; milestone notices | active customer | opens over time, clicks to the product |
| Win-back | expired-trial series, cancelled-customer re-engagement | churned, lost | return rate |
| Campaigns | newsletters, seasonal promotions, product updates, industry notes, pricing announcements | any stage, by segment | clicks, unsubscribes |

## Rules of thumb the source gives

- Onboarding emails are triggered by what the user did or did not do, not
  by the calendar; a reminder that fires after the step is done is a
  bug.
- Usage summaries earn their place only while the numbers change; a
  summary that says the same thing every week trains people to ignore
  the sender.
- Payment recovery is the highest-value automated sequence most teams
  under-invest in; it belongs to billing, and its owner is not marketing.
- Newsletters accept unsubscribes as list hygiene; a rising unsubscribe
  rate on a triggered sequence is a targeting or timing problem.

## Orphans

An email with no family is usually a leftover: a test workflow left on,
a sequence for a product that changed, a campaign that never got an
end date. List orphans separately; they are the easiest overlaps to
remove.
