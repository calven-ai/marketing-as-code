# Journey stages as a synthesis lens

Condensed from the source above. When a synthesis is about "where do
people struggle", sort the evidence by journey stage before by theme;
the same quote means something different at consideration and at
onboarding.

## Six stages and their typical touchpoints

| Stage | The customer is | Typical B2B touchpoints |
| --- | --- | --- |
| awareness | discovering the problem has a name | search results, social, ads, a peer's mention |
| consideration | comparing ways to solve it | blog, review sites, webinars, case studies, a demo form |
| decision | choosing and buying | the sales demo, the pricing page, security review, procurement |
| onboarding | trying to reach first value | welcome emails, setup wizard, implementation calls |
| retention | using it as routine | feature emails, support, QBRs, the product itself |
| advocacy | recommending it | NPS surveys, reference calls, reviews, referrals |

The lifecycle stages in `data/ontology/funnel.md` say where a record is;
these stages say what a person is doing. Do not merge the two.

## Per stage, write down

Customer goal, emotional state, the touchpoints by channel, the friction
named in the evidence, the improvement the evidence suggests, the metric
that would show it, and the drop-off risk.

## Where drop-off shows up

Quantitative: funnel and cohort reports in `data/analytics/snapshots/`.
Qualitative: session recordings, interviews, and a one-question survey at
the stage transition. The source's usual friction: irrelevant content at
awareness, missing social proof at consideration, a complex signup at
decision, an unclear first-value moment at onboarding, no referral path
at advocacy.

## Variants

Journeys differ by role (technical against executive), company size
(self-serve against sales-assisted), how aware the person is
(problem-aware to product-aware) and channel (inbound against outbound).
Tag each verbatim with the variant it came from before generalising.
