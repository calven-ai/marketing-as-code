# Bars, types and scoring

Open this file at steps 4 and 7.

## What counts as an opportunity

A move the company is not making yet: a segment, persona, market or language it does not address; a message it does not lead with; a counter to a named competitor move; a play timed to a dated regulation, deadline or market event; a pricing or packaging response.

Taking a direction the strategy documents already set but the market does not hear yet (a segment they name with no campaign behind it, a message they define that the site does not lead with) is a move, not a repetition: say which part of the documents it puts into market. Restating an existing pillar, value proposition or proof point is not an opportunity.

## The five bars (all must pass)

1. **Grounded.** A cited input supports the move itself, not merely its topic. When a headline or a clipped line cannot carry the claim, read the full source before relying on it.
2. **Specific.** Names who it is for (segment, persona or product) and the concrete PMM action: a campaign, a page, a comparison, a proof point, a launch message, a pricing response. An adjacent audience needs evidence that it fits what the company already sells.
3. **New.** Says plainly how it differs from what the baseline already says, judged at the level of the buyer's decision: a displacement play after a competitor's acquisition is new even if the features are already on the site. "Sharpen", "clarify" or "lead more strongly with" an existing message fails unless it names the missing audience, comparison or proof. A move built only from what the product brief or positioning already say also fails.
4. **Commercial.** States which buyer decision changed and why this move could win evaluations, adoption or competitive preference. A missing explainer, calendar, checklist or guide is not an opportunity on its own; a regulation's name or a new content format is never the whole difference. For a regulatory play, say why buyers facing it should pick this company.
5. **Honest.** Never claims a capability, customer, certification, customer dissatisfaction or urgency the inputs do not support. This bar is about the claim, not about proof: shape the move around what is known (a comparison on strengths the company already claims, a bounded test of a segment or message, a response that names what still needs confirming) and reject only a move whose core promise you would have to invent.

## Type (pick one)

| Type | Use for |
| --- | --- |
| Messaging angle | A campaign or theme, including one aimed at a segment the strategy already prioritizes |
| Positioning shift | A change to the category, frame of reference or primary alternative |
| New segment | A vertical, company size or geography the strategy does not already prioritize |
| Persona expansion | A buyer or user role the company does not address yet |
| Competitive counter | A response to a named competitor's move |
| Pricing & packaging | A pricing, packaging or plan response |
| Use-case play | Marketing an existing capability toward a new use case (solutions marketing), not building anything new |

## Scores

Use the whole scale. The top band is the exception; when torn, pick the lower level.

| Score | Levels |
| --- | --- |
| Impact | **High:** changes how the company goes to market (a repositioning, a new segment, a pricing move); expect at most one or two in a set of about eight. **Medium:** a real gain on the motion it already runs (a sharper message, a stronger proof point); most belong here. **Low:** worth doing when there is room; nothing breaks if it slips |
| Timeline | **Now:** the window closes if the company waits (a competitor move to counter, a live deal pattern, a dated regulation). **Next:** the normal planning cycle, the right default. **Later:** real, but depends on something that has not happened yet. "Now" is not a way to say "important"; that is impact |
| Sizing tier | **Large, Medium or Small**, relative to the other opportunities in the set. Required; use Small when you genuinely cannot size the audience. Add a free-text sizing line with the proxy (for example "about 120 target accounts") and where it comes from, or "not sized" |
