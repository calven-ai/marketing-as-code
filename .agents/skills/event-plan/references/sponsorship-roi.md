# Sponsorship: the math, the checklist, the negotiation order

Condensed from the source above.

## The formula

Cost per qualified meeting = total cost (fee, travel, staff time, booth
build) divided by expected qualified meetings. Compare with the team's
cost per meeting from outbound and ads. If the event is three times the
outbound cost with no strategic upside, pass. The sponsorship has to beat
the best alternative use of the same money, not match it.

## Evaluation checklist

1. Audience overlap in absolute ICP numbers, not total attendance: ask for
   the breakdown by role and company type, audit last year's attendee and
   speaker lists. A 5,000-person event with 200 ICP attendees is a
   200-person event.
2. Meetings math, backwards: a well-worked booth converts 10 to 20 percent
   of relevant walk-bys into real conversations.
3. Strategic multipliers that can justify a premium: buyer concentration
   unavailable elsewhere, a category-defining event, executives who avoid
   cold outreach, credibility.
4. The counterfactual: what the same budget yields in the top channel.

Red flags in a prospectus: attendee counts without composition;
impressions as the headline metric; leads defined as badge scans; a
sponsor roster that does not return.

## Negotiate in this order, before price

1. A speaking or panel slot (stage beats floor).
2. Side-event rights (a dinner, breakfast or workshop during the event).
3. Attendee list access in writing (opt-in scans, a registration question,
   sponsored-session registrants).
4. Placement and timing (near coffee, entrances, the main-stage exit).
5. Price, last; unsold inventory discounts steeply inside eight weeks.

## The side event

8 to 14 curated ICP attendees plus two or three magnetic guest
practitioners; personal invitations two to four weeks out; no pitch. Often
a fraction of a mid-tier sponsorship, deeper meetings with chosen accounts,
and it needs no permission from the organiser. "Join ten `<role>`s for
dinner during `<event>`" outperforms any booth copy.

## Booth operations

Two energetic people beat five tired ones; a shift schedule. A 30-second
qualifying question ("what does your team use for X today?") and a
graceful exit for non-ICP traffic. Fifteen seconds of notes after every
real conversation, within the hour; a scan without context is a name with
amnesia. One message on the booth (the category problem, not a feature
list); a book-a-meeting QR code to a calendar, not a form; meetings
pre-booked so the booth is a venue, not a net.

## Follow-up tiers, 24 to 48 hours

| Tier | Definition | Action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| hot | a real conversation and an agreed next step | personal email same or next day, referencing the conversation, with a calendar link |
| warm | a conversation without a commitment | a personal note and one asset matched to what they said |
| scan only | a badge scan | one light "we were both at `<event>`" touch, or nothing; never a cold sequence |

The person who had the conversation writes or reviews the follow-up.

## Attribution

Tag every touched contact with the event source; measure conversations,
meetings, opportunities, pipeline and closed-won influenced over a 90-day
window (longer for long cycles); capture self-reported "met you at". Report
cost per qualified meeting, cost per opportunity and pipeline influenced;
log scans for logistics only. Budget for a weaker first year and judge the
trajectory into year two.
