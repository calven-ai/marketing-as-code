# Evidence rules for the ICP

Open this when sorting facts into tiers, deciding which source feeds which section, writing questions for the team, or folding the team's answers back in.

## Provenance tiers, field by field

| Field | Website alone | With materials |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Category, value, differentiators, named customers, integrations, stated verticals | Stated | Stated |
| Company size and revenue band | Inferred from the scale of published customers, labelled | Stated if a deck, segment doc or CRM export gives it |
| Smallest winnable account (the floor) | Gap | Stated from closed-won data or win/loss notes |
| Geography | Stated only if the site claims a region; otherwise a brief labelled inference or a gap | Stated from account lists or the CRM |
| Buying triggers | Inferred from case-study narratives, labelled | Stated from win stories and win/loss notes |
| Segment priority | Inferred from evidence strength, labelled | Stated if a segment doc ranks it |
| Disqualifiers / Anti-Profile | Gap: never synthesise from the site | Stated from loss reasons and churned accounts |
| Win and loss reasons, deal size, sales cycle, churn | Gap | Stated from win/loss notes or the CRM |

A labelled inference reads: `_Inferred from published customers: mid-market SaaS, roughly 50 to 500 employees. Confirm against CRM._` A page merely on the same topic is not evidence; a named customer with its scale, a stated segment or a published figure is.

Do not spend searches hunting for the floor, the disqualifiers or private data on a marketing site. They are not there by nature; write the gap marker and move on.

## Which source feeds which section

| Source | Sections |
| --- | --- |
| An existing ICP or segmentation doc | Section for section: bands, tiers, verticals, disqualifiers |
| Account-list rationale, target-firmographic notes | Firmographic Attributes, Segment Tiers, Priority and Secondary Verticals |
| Sales decks, case studies | Use Cases & Pain Points, Behavioral Attributes, named customers in Firmographics and Verticals |
| Win/loss notes | Disqualifiers, Buying Triggers, the smallest-winnable-deal floor |
| CRM export of closed-won and closed-lost | Size and revenue bands, verticals by win count, regions, deal size |
| Customers or case-study pages | Who buys: named customers, their industries and sizes |
| Solutions and use-case pages | Use Cases & Pain Points, who each solution is for |
| About page | Company framing, who it serves |
| Homepage, pricing, integrations pages | Segment hints, Technographic Attributes |

## Reading a CRM export

Count before you claim. Group closed-won accounts by employee band, industry and region and report the counts with the sample size and date range ("31 of 44 closed-won accounts, Jan to Sep 2026, had 50 to 500 employees"). Compare with closed-lost to find disqualifier candidates. A pattern from fewer than ten deals is labelled as a small sample.

## Questions for the team

Ask only what is true of all four: no source already answers it, the ICP owns the fact, the answer would materially change the document, and only the company can know it.

- The ICP owns: company size, industries, regions, buying triggers, disqualifiers, sales motion and deal shape. Questions about buying roles belong to personas; about alternatives and proof, to positioning.
- Self-contained: the reader sees the question without the document. Name the thing inline; never "the alternatives above".
- One fact per question; two facets of the same fact may share one.
- One or two sentences, 35 words at most, plain words, no jargon the user has not used.
- Never name the machinery (scorecard, weights, tiers, criteria, "the Firmographics section"). Never ask them to assign points, rank criteria or write copy.
- If a source answers part of the gap, ask only for the rest and say what you have: "Your deck names design tools as the core market. Is there a company size below which you stop pursuing deals?"
- Confirm an inference only where the section matters: state it and ask whether it is right.
- At most five, heaviest gap first: an empty core section before a weak detail.

## Folding answers back in

The answer is source material, not copy. Take the fact out and drop the framing, hedging and apologies.

- Third person, declarative, present tense. No "we don't have", "I think", "definitely", "at the moment". Never restate the question.
- One fact per cell or bullet, in its shortest form.
- Change only the cell the answer addresses. Re-emit every other row, bullet and citation in the section unchanged. Never collapse a table into a paragraph.
- No citation on the user's own answer; they are the source.
- Do not extrapolate: "we sell to hospitals" fills the cell it answers; it does not create a healthcare vertical, deal sizes or triggers nobody mentioned.

Examples (invented):

- Answer: "Rising ticket costs could be one, because we actually let them skip the extra agents they'd need during outages." Write: `| Rising support costs | Budget review flags incident overtime | Medium |`, not the answer reworded.
- Answer: "We don't have churn data at the moment because we're really early." Write only `_Not tracked yet: no churned customers yet; confirmed 2026-11-03._` in the cell.
- Answer names one new disqualifier; the section already lists four. Write all four unchanged plus the new one.

After folding, reread every section you touched: nothing dropped, format unchanged, no first-person narration, nothing said twice.
