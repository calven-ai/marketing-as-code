# What counts as a change, by surface

Open this in step 2. Each surface has its own bar. For a competitor's change, read "we" as the competitor.

## Pricing

**Stance: presume a change.** Nobody edits a pricing page by accident, and every quote, deck and calculator repeats it. Report every change to what a customer pays or gets.

Report:
- A **price** that moved in either direction, appeared or disappeared.
- A **plan or tier** added, removed, renamed, merged or split.
- **What a tier includes:** seats, usage limits, quotas, overage rates, entitlements moving between tiers.
- The **free tier or trial:** length, existence, card requirement, limits.
- **Billing terms:** annual versus monthly discount, minimum commitment, contract length, payment terms.
- A **"contact sales" flip** either way: a published price going to "talk to us", or a hidden price going public. That is a packaging decision, not layout.
- **Add-ons** and their cost.
- A **new pricing metric:** per seat becoming per usage, per credit, per workflow. The unit changing matters more than the number.

Watch the unit, not just the number: "50 USD per user per month" becoming "50 USD per user per month, billed annually" is a change though the number held. So is "unlimited" becoming a number, or the reverse. Quote the span that carries the unit.

Not a change: testimonial and logo rotation, FAQ rewording that changes no term, layout and colour, a comparison row reordered with its content unchanged.

Kinds: **Price** (what a customer pays changed) · **Packaging** (a tier's contents or structure moved, no price moved) · **Other**.

## Product releases

Changelogs, release notes, what's new pages, product pages, roadmaps. **Stance: triage per release.** These pages exist to list changes, so the question is which entries name a capability rather than describe maintenance.

Report:
- A **new product or module**: something that did not exist and now has a name.
- A **major capability** in an existing product, especially one a seller would demo.
- A capability reaching **general availability**, leaving beta, or entering early access.
- A capability **retired, deprecated or sunset:** as important as a launch to everyone whose deck still shows it, and far easier to miss.
- A **platform or deployment change:** a new region, cloud, runtime, or self-hosted option.
- A **named integration** with a third-party system.
- Anything on a **roadmap page:** a public statement of intent; a rewrite always changes what the market is told.
- A **limit or entitlement** stated here rather than on the pricing page (packaging often lands in release notes first).

Not a change: bug fixes, performance work, in-product copy, dependency bumps, improvements with no named capability. A release of ten fixes and one named feature yields one candidate: the feature. A version number is context, not the fact: "v16.2 released" is not a candidate; what is in it may be. Put the version in the claim and quote the line describing the capability.

Kinds: **Launch** (a product or module) · **Feature** (a capability inside one) · **Integration** · **Retirement** · **Expansion** (a region or deployment target) · **Other**.

## Trust and security

Security pages, trust centres, compliance pages, subprocessor lists. **Stance: presume a change.** These move rarely, a legal or security team signs off on every word, and security questionnaires are answered from them, so a change dates every deck and one-pager that repeats the old claim.

Report:
- A **certification or attestation** gained or lost: SOC 2 (Type I or II), ISO 27001, ISO 42001, HIPAA, PCI DSS, FedRAMP and its level, StateRAMP, C5, IRAP, Cyber Essentials.
- A **subprocessor** added or removed. Name it.
- A **new data region or residency option.**
- A **customer-facing security capability:** SSO, SCIM, audit logs, customer-managed keys, private networking, IP allowlisting.
- A **compliance claim** about a framework or regulation now met.
- A **penetration test, audit cadence or bug bounty** appearing or changing.

Dates matter: an attestation usually states a period ("for the period ending 31 March 2026"). Record it as the effective date; a renewed SOC 2 and a first SOC 2 read the same otherwise, and only one changes what can be claimed.

Not a change: status page incident history, a reworded security philosophy, a badge image swap, a contact address change.

Kinds: **Certification** · **Integration** (a named subprocessor) · **Expansion** (a data region) · **Feature** (a security capability) · **Other**.

## Positioning

Homepage, solution pages, about pages, comparison pages. **Stance: high bar, read as one story.** Marketing pages are rewritten constantly and most of it means nothing; this surface has the highest noise. A repositioning shows up as coherent edits across pages, so prefer one site-level change over many per-page ones. Nothing shipped: record what moved in the pitch, not a product claim. It still matters, because every deck and email repeating the old pitch is now off-message.

Report:
- A **category claim:** the market or frame of reference claimed ("data observability" becoming "data trust").
- The **stated audience:** a segment, industry, role or company size added, dropped or widened.
- A **named product, platform or tier** introduced, renamed or retired.
- A **value claim that carries a fact:** a number, a guarantee, a named outcome.
- A **competitor named,** a comparison introduced, or a migration offer.
- **Proof with a number or a name:** a customer logo, a metric, an analyst placement.

Not a change:
- The same claim reworded. If the claim needs the word "reworded", "refreshed", "tightened" or "clarified", it is not a candidate.
- Sweeping prose with no fact under it ("reimagines", "redefines", "elevates").
- A section moving, a hero image swap, a navigation change.
- A statistic ticking up on a rotating tile (100+ to 120+ connectors is the same claim).

The case to catch: a one-word category change. "Metrics observability" becoming "data observability" looks trivial in a diff and is the most valuable thing this surface yields. Quote both sides.

Kinds: **Category** · **Audience** · **Value claim** · **Naming** · **Competitive** · **Proof** · **Other**.
