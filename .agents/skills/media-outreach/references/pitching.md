# Pitches: anatomy, subject lines, follow-ups, timing

Condensed from the source above. Journalists get 50 to 200 pitches a day
and decide in three to five seconds of scanning.

## Anatomy

| Part | Job | Size |
| --- | --- | --- |
| subject | earn the open | 50 to 60 characters |
| hook | why now, tied to their recent piece | 1 to 2 sentences |
| story | the newsworthy claim with proof | 2 to 3 sentences |
| ask | one specific, easy request | 1 sentence |
| credentials | why you can speak to it | 1 to 2 sentences |
| sign-off | | 1 line |

Under 150 words in all; readable in under a minute; the key facts in the
body, never only in an attachment.

## Subject formulas

Exclusive offer (breaking news, tier 1); data hook ("new data: 80 percent
of X fail"); timely tie-in ("for your X coverage"); contrarian; a
question that invites curiosity; a name drop for executive news.

Avoid: "Press release: Acme announces"; "following up"; "URGENT";
"partnership opportunity".

## Personalisation ladder

| Level | Example | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| none | "Dear journalist" | deleted |
| name | "Hi Sarah" | still reads mass-sent |
| beat | "for your fintech coverage" | shows research |
| article | "your piece on X" | shows reading |
| insight | "building on your X thesis" | the best response rates |

## The three-sentence pitch

Hook connected to their coverage; the claim with a proof point; a
specific, easy ask.

## Follow-ups

| Touch | Day | Content |
| --- | --- | --- |
| pitch | 0 | the full pitch |
| follow-up 1 | 3 to 4 | new information or data |
| follow-up 2 | 7 to 8 | a different hook, same story |
| stop | 10 plus | nurture list; retry with a new story later |

Never "just checking in", "I know you are busy", "bumping this", or the
original forwarded with a question mark. Two follow-ups at most.

## Timing

| Outlet | Best | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| daily news | 6 to 9 in the morning, their time zone | after noon |
| weeklies | Monday to Tuesday | Thursday to Friday |
| freelancers | vary and test | mass-pitch hours |
| podcasts | weekday mornings | recording windows |

Do not pitch into a breaking-news cycle.

## When they decline

Ask for a colleague on the beat or a better angle. Never argue the
newsworthiness.

## Anti-patterns

Mass BCC; a release attached instead of the facts in the body; "per my
last email"; the wrong beat; a vague ask; the hook in paragraph three;
more than two follow-ups.
