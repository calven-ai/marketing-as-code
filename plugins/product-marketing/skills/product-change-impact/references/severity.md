# Severity

Open this in step 7, after the list of recorded changes is final. Every change stays on the list whatever it rates; severity only says how loudly it arrives.

## The question

Severity is how much downstream work a change creates for the people who own the assets that repeat the old story: decks, one-pagers, battlecards, docs, sales emails.

The test: *if a seller ran the usual pitch tomorrow without knowing this, how wrong would they be?*

- **High:** they would say something that is now false. A price, a plan, a capability, a certification or the audience changed.
- **Medium:** they would be off-message. The story moved; the facts did not.
- **Low:** nobody would notice. The same claim, arranged differently or restated elsewhere.

For a competitor's change, ask the same about the competitive pitch: if a seller ran the usual pitch against them tomorrow, how wrong would they be? A battlecard line, a comparison page claim or a landmine question that is now false is High.

When the company sells several products and a change concerns one, rate it for that product's assets: repricing one of several products is still High for the people selling it. A change tied to no product is company-wide.

## Calibration

Rate the shape of the change, not the size of the words.

| Shape of the change | Severity | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A price or pricing metric changed | High | every quote, deck and calculator with the old number is wrong |
| A plan or tier added, removed or renamed | High | packaging is on every one-pager |
| A capability retired, deprecated or sunset | High | it is still being demoed |
| A new product or module shipped | High | the thing being sold changed |
| The stated audience broadened or narrowed | High | who it is sold to changed |
| A certification that unlocks a segment (FedRAMP, HIPAA) | High | closed deals reopen, and questionnaires cite the security page |
| A new region, cloud or deployment option | High | an answer given today is out of date |
| A competitor closes a gap we sell against | High | a claim on the battlecard is now false |
| A capability inside an existing product shipped | Medium | a feature, not a product |
| A named integration added | Medium | worth a mention, rarely a rewrite |
| A general certification (SOC 2, ISO 27001) | Medium | table stakes met |
| The category claim or narrative reworked | Medium | the story moved; nothing sold changed |
| A subprocessor added or removed | Medium | a tell about what is coming, not a shipped thing |
| A marquee customer or quantified outcome added | Medium | strong proof, but proof |
| An existing capability restated on a second page | Low | the same fact, a new surface |
| Detail added to something already recorded | Low | the move already landed |
| Boilerplate, retention wording, a policy clause with no new data category | Low | policy hygiene |

Read the pattern rather than matching words. The line runs between **facts an asset states** and **the way the company talks about itself**. A repositioning can sound enormous and still be Medium; one changed number in a price can sound trivial and be High.

Removals rate as high as additions.
