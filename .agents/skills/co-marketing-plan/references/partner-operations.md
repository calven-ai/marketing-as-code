# Partner operations: lead rules, attribution, cadence, what goes wrong

Condensed from the source above; the parts a single joint campaign needs,
with the programme-level material summarised.

## Lead handling rules to write into the plan

- Which leads go to whom: each side's own registrants by default; shared
  leads only with the consent language on the form saying so.
- A follow-up SLA on each side (the plan names hours, not "promptly").
- Deal registration if either side sells to the other's leads: who
  registers, the protection window (60, 90 or 120 days by deal size), and
  the conflict rule (first to register within 7 days wins; an account with
  an open opportunity stays with its owner unless the partner shows a
  prior relationship; both engaged means co-sell with a split;
  unresolved in 48 hours escalates).

## Attribution, two kinds, tracked separately

| Kind | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| partner-sourced | the partner registered it and the first meeting came from their introduction |
| partner-influenced | we originated it; the partner took part (calls, references, evaluation) |

CRM fields to have before the campaign: `partner_sourced`,
`partner_influenced`, `partner_name`, `deal_registration_id` and its
dates, `partner_tier`. Tag at origin, never retroactively; enforce in the
deal review.

## Co-sell rules for the campaign's follow-up

One designated lead per deal; a weekly sync between the two contacts on
active deals; shared deal notes; an escalation path when a deal stalls.

## Ideal partner profile, for context

Three dimensions scored 1 to 5 each across four criteria: strategic fit
(shared ICP, complementary offer, aligned motion, geography or vertical),
operational readiness (a partner team, a CRM, a customer base, marketing
capability), commitment (an executive sponsor, willingness to invest in
enablement, prior referrals). 36 to 60 is strategic, 24 to 35 growth, 12
to 23 referral, under 12 not ready.

## Enablement content a partner needs

A product one-pager, the ICP and persona guide, a battlecard, a demo
environment, a co-branded deck, case studies, an ROI calculator, an
objection guide, a pricing guide (restricted). The `sales-enablement-kit`
skill writes these.

## Cadence

Weekly: the registration pipeline and active co-sell deals (15 minutes).
Monthly: activity across partners; inactive ones flagged. Quarterly with
strategic partners: revenue and pipeline review, feedback, account-map
refresh, next quarter's plan, executive alignment. Yearly: tiers,
economics, programme return.

## Ramp expectations

First deal registration inside 60 days; first closed deal inside 120;
activation above 60 percent in the first 90 days. Meaningful revenue
takes 6 to 12 months; watch registrations and pipeline first.

## Pitfalls

Fifty recruited, none producing (measure activation; ten active beat
fifty dormant); fights over accounts (registration rules); partners who
do not understand the product (enablement, not self-service); untrackable
partner revenue (the CRM fields above); equal investment, unequal results
(tier and invest accordingly; retire after two idle quarters); bad-fit
leads from partners (qualification criteria they are trained on); "no
return yet" (leading indicators first).
