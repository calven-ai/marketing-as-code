# Ideal Customer Profile

The summary, the firmographic attributes and the disqualifiers are filled;
the rest waits for the first campaign that needs it. The people inside
these accounts live in the personas file.

## ICP Summary

> Our best-fit customer is a **SaaS company with 50 to 500 employees** in
> **design, collaboration and developer tools**, with **a Head of Support
> who owns the support tool budget**, running **a monitoring suite whose
> bundled status page is visibly not enough**, facing **a recent incident
> that produced a ticket spike or an escalation from a large account**.

## Firmographic Attributes

| Attribute | Ideal range | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Company size | 50 to 500 employees | A support team of five or more, and a budget owner for its tools |
| Annual revenue | 5 million and up | Supports the price and means customers worth keeping |
| Industry | Design, collaboration, developer tools | The product is open all day; outages are felt in minutes |
| Geography | North America and Europe | English-first support, two regions to segment |
| Org structure | A Head of Support or Operations separate from engineering | The buyer is not the person who set up monitoring |
| Operational footprint | Customers on more than one plan tier or region | One message for everyone is wrong for most of them |

## Use Cases & Pain Points

- **The blank page during an incident:** nobody knows what to write, so
  the first update goes out late and sounds like a lawyer.
- **The wrong audience:** every customer gets the alarm, including the ones
  who were never affected, and the tickets follow.

## Buying Triggers / Signals

- An incident in the last quarter that produced a ticket spike or an
  escalation from a large account
- A renewal conversation that started with "about that outage"

## Segment Tiers

| Tier | Definition | Fit criteria |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tier 1: Core | Design, collaboration or developer tool, 50 to 500 people, recent incident | Firmographics plus a trigger in the last quarter |
| Tier 2: Expansion | Same size, another SaaS vertical, or no recent trigger | Meets the firmographics, missing the trigger or the vertical |
| Tier 3: Opportunistic | Under 50 people, or over 500 with a support team that buys its own tools | Interest without the budget shape we sell to |

## Priority Verticals

Design tools, collaboration tools, developer tools.

## Disqualifiers / Anti-Profile

- **Under 20 employees:** no support team, and the founder writes the
  outage email; a good-enough status page is right for them.
- **Support does not own its tools:** if engineering buys everything, the
  bundled page wins on price and we lose on budget, not on product.
- **Internal tools only:** no external customers to communicate with.
- **No executive sponsor:** a support agent who loves the product but has
  no budget owner behind them stalls at procurement.
- **Wants only the status page:** we are not the cheapest page; we are the
  whole conversation.
