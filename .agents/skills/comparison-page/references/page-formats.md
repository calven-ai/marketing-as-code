# Comparison and alternative page formats

Condensed from the competitors skill in marketingskills. Four formats,
each for a different search intent; pick by the keyword, not by
preference.

## The four formats

| Format | URL pattern | Intent | Keywords |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Singular alternative | `/alternatives/acme` | Actively switching from one tool | "acme alternative", "alternative to acme" |
| Plural alternatives | `/alternatives/acme-alternatives` | Researching options | "acme alternatives", "best acme alternatives" |
| Us vs them | `/vs/acme` | Head-to-head comparison | "us vs acme", "acme vs us" |
| Them vs them | `/compare/acme-vs-beta` | Comparing two competitors; we are the informed third option | "acme vs beta" |

Structures:

- Singular: validate the pain, position, detailed comparison, who should
  switch, migration, social proof, call to action.
- Plural: the pains, evaluation criteria, four to seven genuine
  alternatives (us first, but real competition), comparison table,
  recommendations by use case.
- Us vs them: short verdict, comparison table, category breakdowns,
  honest positioning for both, testimonials, migration support.
- Them vs them: both products, category comparisons, who each serves, our
  positioning as the alternative, a three-way table.

## Fairness rules

Acknowledge their strengths; be accurate about our limits; never
misrepresent a feature. Readers verify. Explain why a difference matters,
not only that it exists, and say which customer each tool fits.

## Sections every page carries

A two or three sentence summary of the key differences; a comparison
table; paragraph comparisons that say when each difference matters;
feature and pricing breakdowns by tier with total cost; "who it's for"
for each option; migration (what transfers, what needs reconfiguring, a
switcher's words); testimonials from people who switched.

## Research that has to happen first

Use the product; verify current pricing and hidden costs; read reviews on
the major sites; talk to switchers in both directions; read their own
comparison pages. Keep one profile per competitor as the single source
(here: the battlecard), and refresh pricing and major features quarterly,
immediately when a customer flags a change, fully once a year.

## Search and AI answers

Plural alternative pages earn citations in AI answers but rarely a
recommendation unless reviews, forums and analysts agree offsite; an
emerging brand may see its competitors surface through its own page.
Publish anyway: the page frames the intent. Link comparison pages to each
other, build a hub page, and add FAQ markup for the common questions.

## Checklist

Before: gather value proposition, differentiators, pricing, honest
limits; map the landscape and the volumes; define the goal. Per page:
research, summary, tables and paragraphs, who it's for, switcher quotes,
migration notes. After: internal links, schema, a quarterly check.
