# Categories, value levels and proof scores

Open this file when you tag a quote (steps 4 and 5) and when you rate and score (steps 10 and 11).

## Customer categories (pick exactly one)

| Category | What it sounds like |
|---|---|
| Pain | Friction in how they work today, and what it costs them. |
| Gain | A benefit they have felt, or credit to a product they use or are evaluating, stated against what they had before. A benefit they want but do not have yet is a Goal or a Job to be done. |
| Job to be done | The outcome they are hiring software to produce, described without naming a product. |
| Goal | The target they are working to, usually dated or numbered. |
| Objection | A reason not to buy, or not yet: doubt, blocker, missing capability, bad timing. |
| Competitor mention | A rival named: the incumbent, a shortlist entry, or the thing they left. |
| Buying trigger | The event that started the search: a renewal, an incident, a new hire, a mandate. |

A loss reason is still an Objection or a Competitor mention; the deal outcome belongs to the conversation description, not the category.

Sentiment, separately: praise, wins and wanted outcomes are positive; pains, objections and losses are negative; a factual statement with no lean is neutral.

## Vendor categories (pick exactly one)

| Category | What it sounds like |
|---|---|
| Value claim | What we say the product does for them, put as a benefit, not a feature. |
| Differentiation | How we are not the alternative: a named rival, the incumbent, or spreadsheets and meetings. |
| Proof point | The evidence behind a claim: a customer story, a number, a benchmark, a named logo. |
| Discovery question | A question that qualified the deal or opened the problem and got a real answer. |
| Objection handling | The answer to pushback: a reframe, a concession plus a counter, a targeted proof point. |
| Pricing | Price, packaging, tiers, seats, term, discounting. |
| Caveat | A limit conceded in our own words: not built yet, not a fit, on the roadmap, only works if. |
| Next step | What we committed to send, do or schedule, and by when. |

A vendor quote must carry a claim a messaging lead would quote back as "this is what we tell buyers". "It's available as a Slack app and you can ask it things" narrates a feature; skip it. If the rep goes on to say "so nobody has to leave Slack to get an answer", that is the value claim.

## Value levels (every quote)

- **critical**: the call's standout evidence, the quote a marketer would build a slide around. A quantified outcome or cost, a named rival with the reason, a decisive objection or trigger, a proof point with a customer and a number, a sharp differentiation line, or a line so well put it would be quoted word for word ("it's a status page with a mailing list bolted on").
- **high**: clearly useful and specific to this call: a concrete pain, goal or claim, a detail only this buyer would give, a real concession or pricing term from our rep.
- **medium**: relevant and accurate but generic, a point many buyers make in the same words.
- **low**: not worth keeping: agreement, restating the other side, feature narration, scheduling, a fragment, a line that only means something next to the dialogue.

A quote that still reads like raw transcription is at most medium, and low if a reader cannot follow it without the recording.

**Repeats:** two quotes from the same side making the same point: keep the stronger. Same topic is not same point: a cost and its cause, two stakeholders' positions, or a condition and the commitment that answers it are distinct. A customer quote never repeats a vendor quote.

**Order** by marginal value: one quote per load-bearing moment first (trigger, quantified cost or outcome, each rival and why, blockers, decision process and deadline, pricing; on our side the sharpest claim, its proof, concessions, commitments), then second angles on the strongest. A fifth pain on the same theme ranks below the first objection. Never promote a weak quote just to cover a moment.

## Publishable (customer quotes only)

Mark a quote publishable only if it could go on the user's website or a battlecard **as proof for the user's company**: a result they got from us (ideally with a number), why they chose us over a rival, or strong, specific praise of the product. It must stand alone for a reader with no context.

Never publishable, however sharp: a pain, an objection, a deadline or budget, talk of a loss or of price, where the deal stands, a rival's strength, a feared risk, praise of a competitor or of a person, a third-person summary, a quote naming an exact price.

Proof type, set only when literally true: **Quantified outcome** (a measured result they got, never a date, price, budget or hoped-for saving), **Competitive win** (they chose us over a named alternative, never a deal still open), **Specific praise**.

## Proof score, 1 to 100 (publishable quotes)

Score each quote on its own; a score never depends on the others.

- **90 to 100**: a concrete result from us, ideally with a number, or a clear win over a named rival; vivid; short enough for a homepage; needs no context.
- **70 to 89**: specific, credible praise or outcome, but no number, a little long, or needs a little context.
- **40 to 69**: positive but generic ("it works well"), or needs context to make sense.
- **1 to 39**: weak as proof: vague, hedged, about a person, about the deal process, praise of a rival, or a summary rather than something a customer would say.

Use the whole range. Most quotes are not 90 or above.
