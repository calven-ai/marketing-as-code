# Speaking: the CFP, the talk, the recording

Condensed from the source above.

## The pitch

Pitch the audience's takeaway, not the company. Title formula: a specific
outcome, a specific audience, a tension or a number ("How we cut CAC 40
percent by killing our best channel", not "Rethinking growth").

Abstract under 150 words, three parts: the problem as the attendee lives
it; two or three concrete takeaways; proof you have done it (numbers,
documented experience). Every number must trace to `strategy/positioning.md`
or a `data/` snapshot.

The ladder: local meetups, niche conference tracks, main stages, larger
recorded platforms. Off-cycle: a short note to organisers with a tight
topic and proof of speaking lands panels and replacement slots more often
than expected.

## Designing the talk

Pass 1, the outline, four things locked in writing: the audience (one
specific person and what they already believe), the Monday takeaway (an
action they can take that week), your win (pipeline, credibility, hiring),
and the blocks, each with a point and its proof.

Pass 2, the emotional storyboard: five to eight beats per 20 minutes, each
with the room's energy, the audience's feeling, and the one sentence the
beat must land. Check: does the sequence earn the takeaway or merely state
it? Cut beats that repeat a feeling.

## The recording is the asset

Talks get transcribed and transcripts get cited. Say the positioning aloud
("we build X, the Y for Z"), speak the numbers instead of pointing at
slides, and put quotable lines at the energy peaks. Confirm recording and
republication rights before accepting; if the event does not record,
re-deliver and record within a week.

## Before, during, after

Before: post why the topic matters now; invite specific attendees to the
session; meet the other speakers (the strongest networking lane). During:
end with a memorable URL and a related asset; write down every Q&A
question. After, within 24 to 48 hours: publish the recording and a
written version; clip the peaks; follow up with the people who asked
questions and approached (the warmest leads). Keep the core talk for a
season; retire it when the Q&A stops teaching you anything.
